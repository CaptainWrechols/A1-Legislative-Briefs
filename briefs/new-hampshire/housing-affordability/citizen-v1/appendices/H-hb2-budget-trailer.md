# Appendix H — Inside the budget trailer (HB2)

New Hampshire enacts much of its housing policy inside HB2, the omnibus policy bill that accompanies each two-year budget. Every extracted section of the last three trailers was screened against the housing terms and hand-reviewed; this appendix lists the housing sections kept. **All votes on HB2 are on the whole trailer, never on one section.**

## HB2 2021 — Laws of 2021, Chapter 91

468 sections; 6 housing-relevant after review. Final passage (whole bill): Senate 14–10 (2021-06-24); House 198–181 (2021-06-24).

| Section | What it does | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 91:23 | Suspended the state's congregate housing and congregate services programs for older adults (Medicaid waiver services) for the 2022-2023 budget years - a service cut, not an expansion. | adjacent |
| 91:117 | Created a study commission on business tax credit carry-overs that was told to examine letting companies put unused credit refunds into affordable housing development. | adjacent |
| 91:195 | Created the Office of Planning and Development inside the Department of Business and Economic Affairs - the state office that helps cities and towns with land use planning and housing data. | adjacent |
| 91:240 | Directed part of the state's renewable energy fund toward community solar projects that serve manufactured-housing communities and multi-family rental housing. | adjacent |
| 91:376 | Put $25 million into the state's Affordable Housing Fund (run by New Hampshire Housing) to finance or match funding for affordable housing projects. | core |
| 91:408 | Put $6 million into transitional housing beds - raising the rates paid for the beds and funding new ones for patients leaving New Hampshire Hospital and people with complex behavioral health needs. | core |

## HB2 2023 — Laws of 2023, Chapter 79

612 sections; 13 housing-relevant after review. Final passage (whole bill): House 326–53 (2023-06-08).

| Section | What it does | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 79:39 | Wrote the InvestNH fund and program into law - a standing fund that makes grants and loans to speed up municipal approvals and support housing construction. (InvestNH began in 2022 with federal money; this made it permanent in statute.) | core |
| 79:43 | Put another $25 million into the Affordable Housing Fund, non-lapsing, for financing or matching funds for affordable housing. | core |
| 79:196 | Restored $1.5 million for the congregate housing and congregate services programs for older adults that the 2021 budget had suspended. | adjacent |
| 79:239 | Within a package of Medicaid rate increases, raised the housing reimbursement rates paid on behalf of people receiving community mental health services. | adjacent |
| 79:301 | Repealed the Board of Manufactured Housing (RSA 205-A:25-31) effective September 1, 2023 - the state board where manufactured-home park residents and park owners could resolve disputes without going to court. A separate bill to restructure the board (SB203) died between the chambers the same year. | core |
| 79:371 | Created a land use review docket in the superior court - a dedicated court track for appeals of local planning and zoning decisions, intended to speed up housing-related land disputes. | core |
| 79:462 | Created the New Hampshire Housing Champion program: cities and towns that volunteer to adopt housing-friendly regulations, train land boards, and invest in water/sewer capacity earn a designation that unlocks state grants. This enacted the substance of SB145, which had passed the Senate 21-3 as a standalone bill. | core |
| 79:464 | Created the state staff positions to run the Housing Champion program. | core |
| 79:465 | Set up the dedicated-fund accounting so Housing Champion money stays in its own fund. | core |
| 79:466 | Funded the Housing Champion program: $5 million for the grant fund plus $250,000 for administration. | core |
| 79:545 | Extended the 2021 budget's $6 million transitional-housing-beds appropriation so it would not expire until mid-2025. | core |
| 79:564 | Put $8 million into raising the rates the state pays homeless shelter programs, plus $2 million for shelter assistance including cold-weather shelter and hotel placements. | core |
| 79:579 | Ordered New Hampshire Housing to study a graduated, proportional rental assistance voucher for people who do not qualify for existing programs, reporting by November 2023. | adjacent |

## HB2 2025 — Laws of 2025, Chapter 141

461 sections; 13 housing-relevant after review. Final passage (whole bill): Senate 16–8 (2025-06-26); House 184–183 (2025-06-26).

| Section | What it does | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 141:31 | Reorganized the state planning office into a Division of Planning and Community Development that administers the state's planning, broadband, and housing programs. | adjacent |
| 141:45 | Directed $5 million a year for two years from the opioid abatement trust fund to year-round emergency shelter for people with opioid use disorder, with services aimed at recovery and permanent housing. | core |
| 141:56 | Funded congregate housing and congregate services for older adults at $350,000 a year for two years. | adjacent |
| 141:212 | Created the Partners in Housing program: state funding for workforce housing built on city-, town-, or county-owned land that is transferred to developers, with at least 20 percent of units meeting workforce-housing rules. | core |
| 141:213 | Let municipalities fast-track site plan review for residential projects on state-listed surplus properties, and let solely residential workforce-housing projects use the quicker 'minor site plan review' where a town has adopted it. | core |
| 141:214 | Extended the Housing Champion fund's purpose to also pay for compiling the list of municipal and county property suitable for residential development. | core |
| 141:335 | Shrank the Housing Appeals Board - the state board that hears appeals of local zoning and planning decisions - from 3 members to 2. | core |
| 141:336 | Attached the Housing Appeals Board to the Board of Tax and Land Appeals for budget and administration, with shared staff. | core |
| 141:337 | Changed Housing Appeals Board members from fixed terms to serving 'at the pleasure of' the governor and Executive Council, who also pick the chair. | core |
| 141:338 | Set a tie-breaker rule for the now 2-member Housing Appeals Board: a member of the Board of Tax and Land Appeals casts the deciding vote. | core |
| 141:340 | Adjusted the Board of Tax and Land Appeals quorum rules to fit its new role backing up the Housing Appeals Board. | adjacent |
| 141:355 | Extended the 2023 Housing Champion appropriation ($5 million) so it would not lapse until mid-2026. | core |
| 141:418 | Ordered the state to renew and fully implement a Medicaid benefit paying for supportive housing services, with progress reports to the legislature in 2025 and 2026. | core |
