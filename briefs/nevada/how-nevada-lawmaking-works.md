# How Nevada Lawmaking Works

A plain-language guide to the lawmaking terms used in The Forum's four Nevada citizen briefs. The same guide appears in each brief's glossary.

## Legislative process guide

- **AB / SB:** Assembly Bill / Senate Bill — the chamber a bill started in, plus its number. Numbering restarts every session, so the same number in different years is a different bill.
- **Amendment / concurrence:** A change made to a bill along the way; if one chamber amends, the other must vote to accept — "concur in" — the change. Bills can die awaiting that one last vote.
- **Appropriation:** A bill or provision that sets aside state money for a purpose.
- **Chambers:** The Legislature's two houses: the Assembly (42 members) and the Senate (21). A bill must pass both to reach the governor.
- **Committee / first committee:** A small panel of lawmakers that studies a bill and votes on whether to send it onward; most bills that fail die in their first committee — "never heard" means no hearing at all.
- **D / R:** Democrat / Republican, shown after a legislator's name; "still serves" and "has left" reflect the current roster.
- **Delivered / signed / chapter:** A passed bill is delivered to the governor; a signed bill becomes a numbered "chapter" of state law, and "no signature, no chapter" means it never became law.
- **Died at session's end / out of time:** A bill that cleared every vote taken but ran out of calendar before the final step — in these briefs, a mark of proven support, not rejection.
- **Floor vote / floor calendar:** A vote of the full chamber, and the schedule of bills waiting for one; a bill "dies on the calendar" when the session ends before its turn — a scheduling death, not a defeat.
- **Interim committee / interim study:** Panels that meet between sessions to study a topic and prepare recommendations for the next session.
- **Money committees / the money stage:** Assembly Ways and Means and Senate Finance review anything that costs money; bills with price tags routinely stop there without a vote against the policy itself.
- **Second house:** After passing the chamber where it started, a bill repeats the entire process — committee, then floor — in the other chamber.
- **Session:** The Legislature meets for 120 days in odd-numbered years (2019, 2021, 2023, 2025); a bill that has not finished every step by then is dead and must start over in a future session.
- **Special session:** A short extra meeting the governor calls for specific topics; bills that ran out of regular-session time can pass there.
- **Sponsor:** The lawmaker or committee that formally introduces a bill; a committee-sponsored bill has no single author. Cross-party sponsorship — sponsors from both parties — is one of the strongest signals a bill will pass.
- **Statute / enacted:** A statute is a law on the books; an enacted bill is one that became law. "By rule, not statute" means an agency or local authority adopted something the Legislature has not.
- **Two-thirds majority:** The constitution requires a two-thirds vote in each chamber — 28 of 42, 14 of 21 — for any measure raising revenue, including fees; a bill can win a majority and still fail this bar.
- **Veto:** The governor's rejection of a bill that passed both chambers. A vetoed bill does not become law unless two-thirds of each chamber votes to override the veto.
- **Vote pairs such as "42–0 and 21–0":** Floor votes are yes–no tallies, one per chamber; 42–0 is a unanimous Assembly vote, 21–0 a unanimous Senate vote.
