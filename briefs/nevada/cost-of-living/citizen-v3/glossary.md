# Glossary — Health Care Costs Brief

Plain-language definitions for the terms and acronyms in "The Rising Cost of Living in Nevada: Health Care" (citizen brief v3.0). Lawmaking-process terms are in the shared companion, "How Nevada Lawmaking Works."

## Health care and insurance terms

*Subject terms used in the brief's proposal sections.*

**Pharmacy benefit manager (PBM).** A middleman hired by insurers to run drug benefits — deciding which drugs are covered, negotiating rebates from drugmakers, and setting what pharmacies get paid. A major driver of what patients pay at the counter.

**Gag clause.** A contract term that barred pharmacists from volunteering that paying cash would be cheaper than using insurance; now banned in Nevada.

**Rebate pass-through.** Requiring the discounts drugmakers pay to PBMs to be passed along to the payer, as Nevada requires for Medicaid.

**340B.** A federal program letting hospitals and clinics that serve low-income patients buy drugs at deep discounts.

**Prior authorization.** An insurer's requirement that a doctor get approval before a treatment or prescription is covered.

**Step therapy.** An insurer's requirement that a patient try cheaper drugs first — and "fail" on them — before the prescribed one is covered.

**Licensure compact.** An agreement among states to honor each other's professional licenses, so a provider licensed in one member state can practice in the others. The **Nurse Licensure Compact** is the nursing version — the one compact Nevada has not joined.

**Credentialing.** An insurer's process for vetting and approving a provider to join its network. Slow credentialing keeps new providers from seeing insured patients for months.

**Network / panel.** The providers under contract with an insurer. A provider whose "panel is full" is not accepting new patients; no Nevada law defines when a panel counts as full.

**Out-of-network.** A provider with no contract with the patient's insurer — the source of the highest surprise bills.

**Arbitration.** A neutral process that settles insurer–provider payment disputes outside court. **Forced arbitration** — the vetoed-bill subject — is a policy clause that strips the patient's right to sue.

**ER / urgent care.** Emergency room versus walk-in clinic. ER visits carry hospital-level charges even for minor problems — why participants proposed splitting the billing.

**Telehealth.** Medical care delivered by video or phone; settled Nevada law.

**Reimbursement rate.** What an insurer or government program pays a provider. Low rates are why some providers do not accept Medicaid patients.

**Provider-assessment mechanism.** A fee collected from providers and used to draw down federal matching money — funding higher Medicaid payments without state general-fund dollars.

**Medicaid / CHIP.** The state-federal insurance program for lower-income Nevadans, and its companion program for children. The state sets many of its rules, which is why so many bills target it.

**Medicare.** The federal insurance program for people 65 and over and some people with disabilities. Its payment rates and drug prices are set federally.

**Tricare.** The federal health program for military members, retirees, and their families.

**Public Option.** A state-designed health plan any Nevadan can buy.

**Any-group pooling.** Letting small businesses or unrelated groups band together to buy insurance at large-group prices — never filed as a bill.

**Self-funded employer plan.** A plan where the employer pays claims itself and an insurer only administers paperwork. Most large-employer coverage works this way — and it is beyond state regulation (see ERISA).

**Health Service Corps.** Nevada's loan-repayment program for providers who commit to practicing in underserved areas.

**Residency.** The years of supervised hospital training after medical school — the bottleneck for growing the doctor supply, and the target of Nevada's residency grants.

**Foreign medical graduates.** Doctors trained abroad, whose path to full Nevada licensure was eased in 2025.

**Autism behavior technicians.** Front-line providers of behavioral therapy for autistic children.

**Dental therapy.** A licensed midlevel dental profession — more training than a hygienist, less than a dentist.

**Medical-debt collection.** The lawsuits, garnishments, and credit tactics used to collect unpaid medical bills; bills limiting them were vetoed.

**Nevada Health Authority.** The new state agency that consolidates Nevada's public health-insurance programs.

**Recruitment grant program.** Competitive state grants aimed at critical provider shortages, enacted in the 2025 special session.

## Federal programs and terms

*Terms from the brief's federal-overlap section.*

**No Surprises Act.** The federal law, in force since 2022, protecting patients nationwide from surprise out-of-network emergency bills.

**Medicare-negotiated prices.** Drug prices the federal government negotiates directly with manufacturers under a 2022 law; the first take effect in 2026. Nevada's vetoed price caps were pegged to them.

**Federal prior-authorization rules.** Rules finalized in 2024 imposing decision deadlines on Medicare Advantage, Medicaid, and exchange plans through 2027; Nevada's law covers the state-regulated plans they do not reach.

**ERISA.** The federal Employee Retirement Income Security Act, which puts self-funded employer health plans under federal — not state — regulation.

## Acronyms and abbreviations

*Every shortened form used in the brief.*

**AB / SB.** Assembly Bill / Senate Bill — the chamber a bill started in, plus its number. Numbering restarts every session, so the same number in different years is a different bill.

**D / R.** Democrat / Republican, shown after a legislator's name.

**PBM.** Pharmacy benefit manager.

**ER.** Emergency room.

**EMS.** Emergency medical services — ambulance and paramedic personnel.

**CHIP.** Children's Health Insurance Program.

**HHS.** Health and Human Services, as in the Senate and Assembly HHS committees.

**ERISA.** Employee Retirement Income Security Act (federal).
