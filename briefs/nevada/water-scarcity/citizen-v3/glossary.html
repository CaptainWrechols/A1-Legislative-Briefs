Glossary — Water Scarcity Brief

Plain-language definitions for the terms and acronyms in “Growth, Water Scarcity, and Long-Term Supply in Nevada” (citizen brief v3.0). Lawmaking-process terms are in the shared companion, “How Nevada Lawmaking Works.”

Water and growth terms

Subject terms used in the brief’s proposal sections.

Water right. A legal entitlement to take a set amount of water from a set source. Nevada’s water is fully spoken for in many places, so rights are bought, sold, dedicated, and retired like property.

Water-right dedication. Handing water rights to a utility or authority as a condition of building — the “bring your own water” idea.

Groundwater basin. An underground water source managed as one unit. An over-appropriated basin has more rights on paper than real water; a critical management area is a severely over-pumped basin under stronger state controls.

Buy-and-retire. The state buying water rights and permanently canceling them so the water stays in the ground.

Basin water budget. An accounting of how much water enters a basin versus how much is pumped out.

Old claims. Water claims from before Nevada’s permit system that have never been formally confirmed; “staff for old claims” means hiring people to settle them.

Conjunctive management. Managing rivers, streams, and groundwater as one connected supply instead of separate systems.

Domestic well. A private well serving a single home. In stressed basins, domestic wells must be metered to keep pumping.

Metering / retail metering. Measuring each customer’s actual water use. Without retail meters, homes and businesses pay flat rates no matter how much they use.

Water-loss audit. A utility’s accounting of water lost to leaks or never billed.

State Engineer. Nevada’s chief water regulator, who decides water-right applications and manages basins; head of the Division of Water Resources.

Water authority. A regional agency, such as the Southern Nevada Water Authority, that manages supply for its area. Its rules are not state law.

Moratorium. A temporary ban — such as the local ban on new evaporative-cooling systems.

Evaporative cooling / water-cooled towers. Cooling equipment that works by evaporating large volumes of water — the standard technology for big buildings and data centers in dry climates.

Refrigerants. The chemicals inside non-evaporative cooling equipment — the water-free alternative.

Data center. A warehouse of computer servers, among the largest water and power users a county can attract.

Tax abatement. A discount on a company’s future taxes, granted up front to recruit it to Nevada. “Conditions on abatements” means attaching requirements before the discount is given.

Cloud seeding. Releasing tiny particles into clouds to coax more snow or rain from storms.

Decorative Colorado River grass. Ornamental turf no one walks on, irrigated with Colorado River water; banned in southern Nevada in 2021.

Master plan. A local government’s long-range blueprint for growth.

Drought reserve. Extra water rights a developer must dedicate beyond a project’s needs — 11 percent in the Truckee Meadows (the Reno–Sparks area).

Public-benefit metrics. Measurable commitments — jobs, water savings — that a company would have to hit before receiving an incentive.

Economic Forum. Nevada’s official five-member panel of private-sector experts whose revenue forecast the state budget must use; the brief’s model for a proposed expert water body.

Shut-off protections. Limits on when a utility may disconnect a customer’s water service.

Federal programs and terms

Terms from the brief’s federal-overlap section.

Interstate compact. A binding agreement among states, approved by Congress. The Colorado River is divided among seven states by compact, so Nevada’s share cannot be changed by a state bill.

Bureau of Reclamation. The federal agency that operates the Colorado River’s dams and reservoirs and sets its shortage rules.

Post-2026 operating guidelines. The next set of federal rules for managing the Colorado River, under negotiation now to replace rules that expire in 2026.

Federal drought-response money. Funds Congress appropriated in 2022 that pay Colorado River users for measured water conservation.

Acronyms and abbreviations

Every shortened form used in the brief.

AB / SB. Assembly Bill / Senate Bill — the chamber a bill started in, plus its number. Numbering restarts every session, so the same number in different years is a different bill.

D / R. Democrat / Republican, shown after a legislator’s name.
