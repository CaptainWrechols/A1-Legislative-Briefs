Glossary — Housing Affordability Brief

Plain-language definitions for the terms and acronyms in “Housing Affordability in Nevada” (citizen brief v3.0). Lawmaking-process terms are in the shared companion, “How Nevada Lawmaking Works.”

Housing and development terms

Subject terms used in the brief’s proposal sections.

Accessory dwelling unit (ADU). A small second home on the same lot as a main house — a casita, garage apartment, or backyard cottage. Nevada’s largest jurisdictions must now allow them.

By right. Development a property owner may build without asking for special permission — no rezoning, no discretionary vote. Apartments on commercial land are now by right statewide.

Inclusionary requirement. A mandate that new developments include a percentage of affordable units — never filed in Nevada.

In-lieu fund / in-lieu fee. Letting a developer pay into a housing fund instead of building required affordable units.

Linkage fee. A charge on new residential development, dedicated to affordable housing.

Impact fee. A charge on new development to fund the infrastructure it will need — roads, sewers, schools.

Corporate-purchase cap. An annual limit on how many homes corporations and LLCs may buy — the record’s most-advanced unfinished idea.

Investor registry. A required state register of investor-owned rental homes.

Rent cap / rent control. A legal limit on how much rent can rise; “the cap-ban” refers to mirror-image bills that would forbid cities from adopting any rent control.

Manufactured-home park. A community of factory-built homes whose owners typically rent the lot under them — why the one rent cap that passed targeted parks and the seniors living in them.

All-in pricing. Requiring the advertised rent to be the real total, with mandatory fees included and disclosed — now law.

Application fee / late fee / grace period. The fee to apply for a rental; the penalty for late rent (capped at 5 percent); and the three-day window after the due date before a late fee may be charged.

Landlord registry. A proposed statewide list of rental-property owners.

Eviction diversion. Programs that resolve nonpayment cases — through mediation or rental assistance — before they become evictions.

Voucher holders. Renters who pay part of their rent with a federal housing voucher (often called Section 8).

Matched-savings accounts. Savings accounts where donors match what a family deposits toward a first home.

Rate buy-down. Paying money up front to lower a mortgage’s interest rate; a state pilot for first-home borrowers passed the Senate and stalled.

Tiny house / factory-built housing. Very small homes, and homes built in factories and assembled on site — both now recognized in Nevada law.

At-cost starter homes / resale protections. Homes sold at construction cost to moderate-income buyers, with rules keeping them from being flipped to investors — an untested design.

Attainable housing. Housing affordable to working households; Nevada’s new $133 million account funds it.

Housing Division / bond authority. The state housing agency, which finances homebuyer programs by issuing bonds rather than through session-by-session laws.

Housing Advocate / Housing Liaison. The Housing Division’s public-facing ombudsman role, and the proposed upgrade of it.

Advisory Committee on Housing. The statutory advisory body of builders, citizens, and legislators, with an annual statewide needs report and the right to request one bill draft per session.

Windsor Park relocation act. The state law relocating residents of Windsor Park, a North Las Vegas neighborhood where sinking ground has damaged homes.

Hedge fund. An investment fund pooling money from large investors — shorthand in the corporate-ownership debate for institutional buyers of single-family homes.

Federal programs and terms

Terms from the brief’s federal-overlap section.

Low-Income Housing Tax Credit. The main federal subsidy for building affordable rentals — tax credits investors buy, financing the projects.

HUD block grants. Flexible federal housing and community-development money sent to states and cities.

FHA / VA loans. Federally backed mortgages that let buyers qualify with smaller down payments.

Junk-fee rule. The 2024 federal rule requiring up-front disclosure of mandatory fees — it covers lodging and event tickets, not rental housing.

Acronyms and abbreviations

Every shortened form used in the brief.

AB / SB. Assembly Bill / Senate Bill — the chamber a bill started in, plus its number. Numbering restarts every session, so the same number in different years is a different bill.

D / R. Democrat / Republican, shown after a legislator’s name.

ADU. Accessory dwelling unit.

LLC. Limited liability company — the business structure many investor landlords use.

HUD. U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development.

FHA. Federal Housing Administration.

VA. U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs.
