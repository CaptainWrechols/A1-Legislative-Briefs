Appendix B — Theme scorecards

Each theme groups the curated bills by what they tried to do. The history basket is a description of the past record only — “Often moved before,” “Got support but didn’t finish,” or “Rarely moved before” — never a prediction or a recommendation. Certainty reflects how many bills support the pattern.

Creating a state minimum wage

  • Bills: 24 (all core) · Became law: 0 · Basket: Rarely moved before · Certainty: high
  • What was tried: fixed hourly floors from $10 to $17; phased three-year schedules with automatic inflation adjustments; the recurring “SC Minimum Wage Act” (agency-adjusted floor); two resolutions for advisory ballot questions (2021 S633, 2023 S28); a constitutional amendment (2019 S147); and a wage-board mechanism letting the state workforce agency set pay rates by occupation (2025 H3735).
  • Where they stopped: the first committee, every time. House Labor, Commerce and Industry took 8; House Ways and Means 5; Senate Labor, Commerce and Industry 8; Senate Judiciary 2 (the constitutional amendment and one ballot resolution); House Ways and Means also took the wage-board bill. None was reported out; none received a recorded vote at any stage in eight years.
  • Typical floor vote: none exists.
  • Examples: 2019-20 H3114 ($10.10), 2021-22 S159 (SC Minimum Wage Act), 2023-24 S28 (ballot question), 2025-26 H3735 (wage boards).

Wage floors for specific groups

  • Bills: 11 (4 core, 7 adjacent) · Became law: 2 · Basket: Often moved before (for narrow, targeted floors) · Certainty: medium
  • What passed: 2021-22 S533 (Act 209 of 2022) banned subminimum pay for workers with disabilities and enacted the Employment First Initiative Act — Senate 43–0, House 104–4. 2023-24 S1001 (Act 192 of 2024) guaranteed inmates in private prison-industry programs at least the federal minimum wage — 45–0 and 109–0.
  • The path there: the Employment First idea died quietly in 2019-20 (H4768, first committee), passed both chambers in 2021 (H3244, 106–9 and 44–0) and died in conference, then passed as S533 the next year.
  • Where the rest stopped: general inmate-wage bills died in first committee in every session (2019-20 H5061, 2021-22 H4050, 2025-26 H3559 and H5357).
  • Examples: 2021-22 S533, 2023-24 S1001, 2021-22 H3244, 2025-26 H5357.

Apprenticeships and hiring tax credits

  • Bills: 7 (all core) · Became law: 2 · Basket: Mixed — often moved as tax measures; unfinished as hiring-rule bills · Certainty: medium
  • What passed: 2023-24 S557 (Act 188 of 2024) raised the apprentice income tax credit and extended how long employers can claim it — 42–0 and 111–0. 2023-24 H3605 (Act 13 of 2023), the Earn and Learn Act, connects students to registered apprenticeships for credit — 114–0 and 39–0.
  • Got support but didn’t finish: 2021-22 H3348 (credits for hiring formerly incarcerated people or veterans into apprenticeships) passed the House 106–0 and died in Senate Finance. 2023-24 S859 (skills-based hiring for state jobs) passed the Senate 42–0 and died in House Labor, Commerce and Industry.
  • Where the rest stopped: the 2025-26 refiles of skills-based hiring (H3479, S272) and the credit-tightening bill (2023-24 H4600) died in first committee.

Career training, technical colleges, and workforce programs

  • Bills: 22 (16 core, 6 adjacent) · Became law: 5 · Basket: Often moved before · Certainty: high
  • What passed: SC WINS workforce scholarships (2021-22 H3144, Act 204 of 2022, 105–1 and 40–0); the workforce coordinating council restructure (2021-22 H4766, Act 194 of 2022); the Statewide Education and Workforce Development Act creating a single state workforce office (2023-24 H3726, Act 67 of 2023, 108–5/102–3 and 41–2); the DEW cabinet-agency act (2023-24 H3783, Act 1 of 2023); and the STEM Opportunity Act (2025-26 H3863, Act 142 of 2026, 103–0 and 43–0).
  • Got support but didn’t finish: WINS 1.0 (2019-20 H3576) passed the House 105–0, got a favorable Senate committee report, and ran out of Senate calendar — the identical idea passed two years later. The career-pathways overhaul passed each chamber separately in 2019-20 (H3759, House 100–3; S419, Senate 40–4 after a long floor fight) and both died in the other chamber’s committee when COVID ended the session. 2023-24 H4060 passed the House 108–2 and died in Senate Education. DEW restructuring passed the Senate twice (2023-24 S546, 42–0; 2025-26 S279, 41–0) and died in House committees.
  • Where the rest stopped: first committee (data-oversight, dual-enrollment, workforce-diploma, service-year bills).

Employer incentives tied to pay

  • Bills: 11 (3 core, 8 adjacent) · Became law: 2 · Basket: Mixed — passes inside tax packages; stand-alone designs unproven · Certainty: medium
  • What passed: job development credits — the state’s existing wage-linked hiring incentive — were updated inside broader tax bills in 2021-22 (S901, Act 237 of 2022, 41–0 and 91–0) and 2023-24 (H4087, Act 222 of 2024, 45–0 and 84–12).
  • Not yet voted on: 2025-26 H4603, the Small Business Livable Wage Tax Credit Act — the closest thing on record to the citizens’ proposal — died in House Ways and Means without a vote, as did 2025-26 H4245 (credits for hiring people leaving public assistance). 2021-22 S398 (wage and job obligations on subsidy recipients, with clawbacks) died in Senate Labor, Commerce and Industry.
  • Got support but didn’t finish: 2021-22 H4252 (Redline Enterprise Zone Act) passed the House 103–2 and died in Senate Finance.

Appendix F — Data limits

What this dataset can and cannot say, in plain language.

  1. The set is keyword-discovered and hand-curated. Pass 1 kept every hit from the State House full-text search plus a title/summary scan of the certified 2019–2026 universe (5,744 bills); a hand review kept 133 as the issue set (54 core, 52 adjacent, 27 context). A wage-related bill that used none of the search terms would be missed, though the term list is broad (26 terms).

  2. No committee vote counts exist — anywhere. South Carolina publishes committee outcomes in bill histories (“Committee report: Favorable”), never tally tables. Nothing in this packet states or implies a committee vote count; any such number found elsewhere has no official source.

  3. No party labels. Attaching parties requires joining ballot PDFs to a legislator roster, which was not fetched for this brief. The packet therefore makes no claims about which party supported or opposed anything.

  4. Floor counts are verbatim. Every yes–no figure comes directly from the official chamber vote-history tables. Only passage-type votes (second/third reading, passage of bill) are used as support signals; tabling and amendment motions are procedural and excluded from the support tables.

  5. “Did not pass (session ended)” for 2025-26 bills reflects the record as of the collection date (August 26, 2026), after the 126th General Assembly’s regular calendar ended.

  6. Budget provisos: FY 2020-21 has no enacted Part IB proviso set — the budget bill died in committee during COVID and the state ran on a continuing resolution — so proviso coverage runs FY 2021-22 through FY 2026-27. Proviso vote counts do not exist: floor votes attach to the whole appropriations bill, never one proviso.

  7. The four citizen proposals are Forum process input. They come from Phase 2 Community Conversations (June–August 2026) and are labeled [P-…]; they are what participants proposed, not verified facts or Forum positions.

  8. No agency outcome data. This packet maps the legislative record only. It does not measure whether enacted programs (WINS, Earn and Learn, apprenticeship credits) actually raised wages; that would need agency and labor-market data outside this dataset.

Appendix H — Citizen proposals and the record

The four proposals below came out of the Forum’s Phase 2 Community Conversations (June–August 2026). They are process input — what participants proposed, with the frequency and consensus noted at the events — not verified facts or Forum positions. Each is matched against the 2019–2026 legislative record. A proposal with zero matched bills is a finding, not a failure: it means lawmakers have not yet tried it.

[P-raise-minimum-wage] Raise the minimum wage / livable wage

  • From the conversations: raise the state minimum wage toward a livable wage. High frequency (3 events); mixed consensus; small-business cost concerns were the main counterpoint.
  • Matched bills (24): 2019-20 — H3114, H3217, H3395, H3467, H4154, S147 (constitutional amendment), S149; 2021-22 — H3018, H3184, H3341, H3480, H3675, S159, S343, S633 (ballot question), S634; 2023-24 — H3805, H5187, S216, S28 (ballot question), S291; 2025-26 — H3226, H3735 (wage boards), H3809.
  • Record: none reported out of first committee; no recorded vote at any stage in eight years.
  • Near misses: S533 (2022) and S1001 (2024) enacted wage floors for specific groups (disability subminimum ban; inmate private-industry floor).
  • Venue note: the federal $7.25 floor applies; state law blocks local minimums, so the General Assembly is the only in-state venue.

[P-age-bracketed-minimum-wage] Age-bracketed minimum wage with a separate youth wage

  • From the conversations: a higher minimum for adult workers (18+) and a separate, lower youth wage for teens. High frequency (3 events); high consensus for a higher adult wage; the open questions were where to draw the age line and whether a youth wage displaces adult workers.
  • Matched bills: none. No 2019–2026 bill proposes a youth wage, training wage, or any age-based bracket; “youth wage” and “training wage” return zero hits in every bill’s full text.
  • Nearest record: S533 (Act 209 of 2022) removed a subminimum tier (for workers with disabilities) rather than creating one.
  • Venue note: federal law already permits a $4.25 youth training wage for a worker’s first 90 days; any state bracket would sit on top of that and of a state minimum that does not yet exist.

[P-workforce-development] Apprenticeships, career pathways, and technical training

  • From the conversations: expand apprenticeships, career pathways, and technical-college training so workers can move into higher-paying jobs. Medium frequency (Greenville, Columbia); high consensus; funding and employer participation were the practical questions.
  • Matched bills (23) — the record’s most successful lane. Became law: H3144 (WINS scholarships, Act 204 of 2022), H4766 (workforce coordinating council, Act 194 of 2022), H3605 (Earn and Learn, Act 13 of 2023), H3726 (Statewide Education and Workforce Development Act, Act 67 of 2023), S557 (apprentice tax credit increase, Act 188 of 2024), H3863 (STEM Opportunity Act, Act 142 of 2026). Got support but didn’t finish: H3576 (2019-20, House 105–0), H3759/S419 (2019-20 career-pathways overhaul, each passed its own chamber), H4060 (2023, House 108–2), S859 (2024, Senate 42–0), H3348 (2022, House 106–0). Died in first committee: H4022, S650, H3757, H3611, S461, H3197, H4984, H3225, H4600, H3479, S272.
  • Budget overlap: the apprenticeship lead-agency role, WINS funding, and critical-workforce-needs funding live in one-year budget provisos (Appendix G).

[P-employer-living-wage-incentives] Government incentives/bonuses for employers paying a living wage

  • From the conversations: state incentives or bonuses for employers who pay a living wage. Medium frequency; mixed consensus; how to fund the incentives was disputed.
  • Matched bills (3): H4603 (2026, Small Business Livable Wage Tax Credit Act — the direct precedent; died in House Ways and Means without a vote), H4245 (2025, credits for hiring and retaining people leaving public assistance; same committee, same result), S398 (2021, wage and job obligations on subsidy recipients; died in Senate Labor, Commerce and Industry).
  • Adjacent record: wage-linked employer incentives already exist and pass when updated inside tax packages — job development credits (S901, Act 237 of 2022; H4087, Act 222 of 2024) and the apprentice credit (S557, Act 188 of 2024).
