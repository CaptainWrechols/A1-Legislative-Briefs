# Appendix H — Citizen proposals and the record

The three proposals below are the **final proposal grid** for Slow Wage
Growth ("SC1 – Slow Wage Growth"), the set that advanced out of the Forum's
Community Conversations. They are **process input** — what participants
proposed, with the frequency, consensus, and concerns reported at the events
— not verified facts or Forum positions. (The earlier Phase 2 working set
carried a standalone "raise the minimum wage" item; the final grid folds it
into the age-bracketed proposal, whose consensus covers raising the adult
minimum.) Each proposal is matched against the 2019–2026 legislative record.
A proposal with zero matched bills is a finding, not a failure: it means
lawmakers have not yet tried it.

## Age-bracketed minimum wage with a separate youth wage

- **From the conversations:** raise the minimum wage for workers over 18, so
  independent adults earn more than teenagers with an after-school job, with
  a separate youth wage. Frequency high (three of four in-person events);
  consensus high. Concern raised: it could limit upward mobility of younger
  South Carolinians.
- **The age-bracket half — matched bills: none.** No 2019–2026 bill proposes
  a youth wage, training wage, or any age-based bracket; "youth wage" and
  "training wage" return zero hits in every bill's full text, and a scan of
  all 24 minimum-wage bills finds no age-based rate language in any of them —
  each sets one floor for all covered workers.
- **The adult-raise half — matched bills (24):** 2019-20 — H3114, H3217,
  H3395, H3467, H4154, S147 (constitutional amendment), S149; 2021-22 —
  H3018, H3184, H3341, H3480, H3675, S159, S343, S633 (ballot question on a
  $15 minimum), S634; 2023-24 — H3805, H5187, S216, S28 (ballot question on
  a $15 minimum), S291; 2025-26 — H3226, H3735 (wage boards), H3809. None
  reported out of first committee; no recorded vote at any stage in eight
  years.
- **Nearest enacted law:** S533 (Act 209 of 2022) and S1001 (Act 192 of
  2024) enacted wage floors for specific groups; S533 removed a subminimum
  tier (for workers with disabilities) rather than creating one.
- **Venue notes:** the federal $7.25 floor applies; federal law already
  allows a $4.25 youth wage for workers under 20 in their first 90 days;
  state law blocks local minimums, so the General Assembly is the only
  in-state venue.

## Workforce development: apprenticeships, career pathways, and technical training

- **From the conversations:** expand apprenticeships, career pathways, and
  technical-college training so workers can move into higher-paying jobs.
  Frequency medium (Greenville, Columbia); consensus high. Concerns raised:
  the programs mostly helped large businesses like Boeing and BMW, and there
  was little awareness of such programs.
- **Matched bills (23) — the record's most successful lane.** Became law:
  H3144 (WINS scholarships, Act 204 of 2022), H4766 (workforce coordinating
  council, Act 194 of 2022), H3605 (Earn and Learn, Act 13 of 2023), H3726
  (Statewide Education and Workforce Development Act, Act 67 of 2023), S557
  (apprentice tax credit increase, Act 188 of 2024), H3863 (STEM Opportunity
  Act, Act 142 of 2026). Got support but didn't finish: H3576 (2019-20,
  House 105–0), H3759/S419 (2019-20 career-pathways overhaul, each passed
  its own chamber), H4060 (2023, House 108–2), S859 (2024, Senate 42–0),
  H3348 (2022, House 106–0). Died in first committee: H4022, S650, H3757,
  H3611, S461, H3197, H4984, H3225, H4600, H3479, S272.
- **On the "who benefits" concern, what the record shows:** the enacted
  designs are not written around large employers — SC WINS pays students,
  Earn and Learn connects students, and S557's credit is by its text open to
  any taxpayer employing a registered apprentice, without size or industry
  limits. No enacted program in this record is restricted to large
  manufacturers. How take-up actually distributes across employers is
  outcome data outside this legislative record (Appendix F, item 8).
- **On the awareness concern, what the record shows:** outreach money exists
  as one-year budget rules — FY 2026-27 proviso 25.8 (public-awareness
  campaign on manufacturing and related careers) and proviso 1.94 (SC Future
  Makers/Tallo platform connecting students to employers). No permanent
  statute funds program awareness.
- **Budget overlap:** the apprenticeship lead-agency role, WINS funding, and
  critical-workforce-needs funding live in one-year budget provisos
  (Appendix G).

## Government incentives / bonuses for employers who pay a living wage

- **From the conversations:** state incentives or bonuses for employers who
  pay a living wage. Frequency medium (two of four in-person events);
  consensus mixed — agreement on incentivizing a living wage, divergence on
  funding and on whether the incentive should be a tax credit or a direct
  state bonus.
- **The tax-credit version — matched bills (3):** H4603 (2026, Small
  Business Livable Wage Tax Credit Act — the direct precedent, and the
  record's one design aimed at small businesses specifically; died in House
  Ways and Means without a vote), H4245 (2025, credits for hiring and
  retaining people leaving public assistance; same committee, same result),
  S398 (2021, wage and job obligations on subsidy recipients; died in Senate
  Labor, Commerce and Industry).
- **The direct-bonus version — matched bills: none.** No bill in this record
  proposes direct state bonus payments to employers tied to the wages they
  pay. The nearest grant-type mechanism filed — the Headquarters Relocation
  and Growth Fund (H5471/S1118, 2026), direct grants to businesses — is tied
  to headquarters location, not wage levels, and died in the money
  committees.
- **Adjacent record:** wage-linked employer incentives already exist in the
  tax code and pass when updated inside tax packages — job development
  credits (S901, Act 237 of 2022; H4087, Act 222 of 2024) and the apprentice
  credit (S557, Act 188 of 2024). Every bill in this proposal's set stopped
  in House Ways and Means or Senate Finance — the rooms where the funding
  question participants disagreed on is decided.
