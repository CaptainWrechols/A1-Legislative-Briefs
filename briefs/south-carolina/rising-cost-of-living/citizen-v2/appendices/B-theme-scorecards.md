# Appendix B — Theme scorecards

Each theme groups the curated bills by what they tried to do. The *history
basket* is a description of the past record only — "Often moved before,"
"Got support but didn't finish," or "Rarely moved before" — never a
prediction or a recommendation. *Certainty* reflects how many bills support
the pattern.

## How electric and gas rates are watched and set

- **Bills:** 22 (all core) · **Became law:** 3 · **Basket:** Got support but didn't finish · **Certainty:** high
- **What was tried:** the Ratepayer Protection Act family (utility whistleblower protections; conflict-of-interest rules for regulators; a ban on utilities giving campaign contributions or anything of value to the Public Utilities Review Committee — the legislators' panel that screens Public Service Commission candidates); revolving-door bans stretching one year to three or four; PSC restructuring (seven seats to five, published reasons for decisions on a deadline); rate-increase freezes during emergencies; public election of the PSC; a citizen oversight council funded by the utilities; PSC approval before billing-practice changes.
- **The pattern:** the House passed the accountability bills four times — 2019-20 H4260 (105–1), 2019-20 H4776 (101–0), 2021-22 H4149 (118–0), 2023-24 H3614 (114–0) — and every one died in Senate Judiciary without a vote. Combined House margin: 438–1.
- **What passed:** the election machinery. 2019-20 S996 (Act 181 of 2020) reopened PSC candidate screening (37–0 and 78–32); 2025-26 S271 and H4402 (Acts 74 and 75 of 2025) restarted PSC elections (40–0 / 110–0 and 44–0).
- **Examples:** 2019-20 H4260, 2021-22 H4149, 2023-24 S909, 2025-26 H5283.

## Santee Cooper, the state-owned utility

- **Bills:** 11 (all core) · **Became law:** 3 · **Basket:** Often moved before (as negotiated packages) · **Certainty:** medium
- **What passed:** 2019-20 H4287 (Act 95 of 2019) set up the sale-bids-versus-reform evaluation after the V.C. Summer collapse (Senate 42–1); 2021-22 H3194 (Act 90 of 2021) rebuilt the board and put Santee Cooper's rates under PSC review for the first time (89–26 and 44–1); 2025-26 S51 (Act 73 of 2025) required proposals for finishing the abandoned reactors (43–0 and 111–0).
- **Where the rest stopped:** the outright sale and divestiture bills (2019-20 S678, H5335; 2021-22 S439) never reached a floor vote. 2025-26 S12 (joint ownership of plants with private utilities) passed second reading 25–18 — the record's closest utility floor vote — then was recommitted to Senate Judiciary in February 2026 and never revived.
- **Examples:** 2019-20 H4287, 2021-22 H3194, 2025-26 S12, 2025-26 S51.

## Big energy bills that set the rate rules

- **Bills:** 21 (13 core, 8 adjacent) · **Became law:** 2 · **Basket:** Often moved before (package pattern) · **Certainty:** high
- **What passed:** 2019-20 H3659 (Act 62 of 2019), the Energy Freedom Act — solar-contract review, an electric customer bill of rights, rooftop-solar billing — 110–0 and 46–0. 2025-26 H3309 (Act 41 of 2025), the Energy Security Act — new plant approvals, public-witness rights in rate cases, rate-case schedules — 94–11 and 38–3.
- **Got support but didn't finish:** 2023-24 H5118, the 2024 energy package, passed the House 88–21 and the Senate 40–2, then died in conference after the House refused the Senate's amendments 23–76. Its successor became the 2025 law.
- **Where the rest stopped:** the rate-mechanism bills — Electric Rate Stabilization (2025-26 H3928, S446: yearly rate reviews instead of big rate cases), the Natural Gas Rate Stabilization rewrites filed by Sen. Young every session (2019-20 S907 through 2025-26 S93), performance-based rates (2019-20 S137), storm-cost securitization (three bills) — all died in first committee. S446 got a favorable report and was then recommitted.
- **Examples:** 2019-20 H3659, 2023-24 H5118, 2025-26 H3309, 2025-26 S446.

## Opening the electricity market to competition

- **Bills:** 7 (6 core, 1 adjacent) · **Became law:** 1 (a study) · **Basket:** Rarely moved before · **Certainty:** medium
- **What passed:** 2019-20 H4940 (Act 187 of 2020) created the Electricity Market Reform Measures Study Committee with an independent expert consultant — House 81–31 (the most contested vote of any enacted bill in this set), Senate 41–0.
- **Where the rest stopped:** every market-opening bill died in its first committee without a vote — and all four arrived in 2026: H5439 (retail choice for households), H5440 and S878 (third-party suppliers), H5525 (co-op wholesale market access). The 2019 territory-choice bill (H3344) died the same way.
- **Typical floor vote:** none exists on any market-opening bill.
- **Examples:** 2019-20 H4940, 2025-26 H5439, 2025-26 S878, 2025-26 H5525.

## Utility bills, fees, and shutoffs

- **Bills:** 19 (all adjacent) · **Became law:** 0 · **Basket:** Rarely moved before · **Certainty:** high
- **What was tried:** bans on charging a fee to pay your own bill (three bills); limits on shutoffs in dangerous conditions (two); bans on moving unpaid balances between accounts (three); a senior surcharge exemption; customer-data privacy (three); utility-paid service-line repairs; bans on billing customers for utilities' legal losses (three); data-center cost walls (two, 2026).
- **Where they stopped:** the first committee, every time — House Labor, Commerce and Industry (13) or Senate Judiciary (6). No bill in this theme received a recorded vote in eight years.
- **Examples:** 2021-22 H3280 (shutoff limits), 2023-24 H3157 (payment-fee ban), 2025-26 H5215 (data-center costs), 2019-20 S904 (customer privacy).

## Money skills in school

- **Bills:** 9 (all core) · **Became law:** 0 as bills — but the policy is in force via the budget and a regulation · **Basket:** Got support but didn't finish (then finished by other means) · **Certainty:** high
- **What was tried:** a half-credit personal finance graduation requirement (2019-20 S15 and H4149; 2021-22 S16, S405, H3116); new required topics — student loans, credit, repayment — for the standing financial-literacy program (2019-20 H3199; 2021-22 H3022, H4582).
- **How far it got as bills:** H3199 passed the House 102–10 and died in Senate Education. S16 passed the Senate 44–1 and the House 109–0 in differing versions and died in conference in June 2022.
- **How it became policy anyway:** FY 2022-23 budget proviso 1.101 ordered the State Board of Education to write the half-credit requirement into regulation. Regulation 43-234 (Document 5130) took effect May 26, 2023, binding students who entered ninth grade from fall 2023 (Class of 2027 first). The approval resolution (2023-24 S732) was recommitted and never passed; the regulation took effect under the regulation-review clock regardless.
- **Since then:** no personal-finance bill has been filed in the 125th or 126th sessions.

## Property taxes on homes

- **Bills:** 67 (48 core, 19 adjacent) · **Became law:** 9 · **Basket:** Mixed — narrow fixes often moved; broad relief rarely moved · **Certainty:** high
- **What passed (all near-unanimous):** shutdown penalty delay (2019-20 H3630, Act 96); small rooftop-solar exemption (2021-22 H3354, Act 68); installment payments (2021-22 H3482, Act 69); surviving spouses, heirs' property, and service-fee rules (2021-22 S233, Act 236); disabled veterans' immediate exemption (2023-24 H3116, Act 116, 111–0 and 45–0); estate continuity for the 4% rate (2025-26 H3841, Act 140, 107–0 and 43–0); the Municipal Tax Relief Act sales-tax swap (2025-26 S866, Act 228 of 2026, 32–9 and 96–16); a higher manufacturing-exemption reimbursement cap (2025-26 S439, Act 122); the Lexington County extension (2025-26 H4187, Act 89).
- **What never moved:** 35 bills raising the $50,000 senior/disabled homestead exemption or exempting seniors' homes entirely — all died in committee except 2025-26 S768 ($100,000 and age 60), which passed the Senate 44–0 and died in House Ways and Means. Full elimination with state reimbursement (2025-26 H3378) died in House Ways and Means, where 45 of this theme's 67 bills stopped. The valuation constitutional amendments (2019-20 H3626 → 2023-24 H3809) and the reassessment delay (2023-24 H4910) never had a vote. Three millage-cap bills would have made local tax *increases* easier (simple-majority override); they died in first committee too.
- **Examples:** 2025-26 S768, 2025-26 H3378, 2023-24 H3116, 2025-26 S866.

## Taxes on vehicles and boats

- **Bills:** 13 (11 core, 2 adjacent) · **Became law:** 0 · **Basket:** Rarely moved before · **Certainty:** high
- **What was tried:** exempting one car for seniors (2025-26 H5014 — the broadest); disabled veterans' vehicles (2019-20 H4564; 2023-24 S943); disabled former first responders (2021-22 H4511; 2025-26 H3410); out-of-state service members (2021-22 S1018); legal guardians (2025-26 H4138); church-leased vehicles (two); boats taxed where kept (2023-24 S38) and no double billing (2019-20 H5111); making treasurers accept car-tax payments from people delinquent on other property (2021-22 H3385; 2023-24 H3085).
- **Where they stopped:** first committee, all thirteen — House Ways and Means (7), Senate Finance (5), House Medical, Military, Public and Municipal Affairs (1). Zero recorded votes.
- **Never filed:** statewide elimination of the vehicle property tax.

## Income and sales tax relief

- **Bills:** 16 (all adjacent) · **Became law:** 2 · **Basket:** Often moved before (when leadership carries it) · **Certainty:** medium
- **What passed:** 2021-22 S1087 (Act 228 of 2022) — brackets collapsed, top rate set to fall from 7% to 6% as revenue allows, military retirement exempted — 39–0/40–0 and 110–0. 2025-26 H4216 (Act 110 of 2026) — top rate to 5.21%, a new 1.99% bracket, federal AGI starting point — the closest enacted margins in this set, House 64–47/68–46 and Senate 39–5.
- **Got support but didn't finish:** the House's own 2022 cut (H4880) passed 112–0 and died in Senate Finance when the Senate's S1087 became the vehicle.
- **Where the rest stopped:** flat-tax overhauls (2019-20 H4334; 2021-22 H3393), the Tax Policy Modernization Act (2021-22 S925), baby-supplies sales-tax exemption (2025-26 S742), food-desert credits (three bills), the Working Family Child Tax Credit (2025-26 H5477), and retirement-deduction increases — first committee, no votes.
- **Budget note:** provisos 117.208 (FY 2025-26) and 117.191 (FY 2026-27) put rate cuts into effect faster than the statutes' revenue triggers.

## Housing costs and rent

- **Bills:** 45 (all adjacent) · **Became law:** 6 · **Basket:** Mixed — financing tools moved; tenant-cost rules rarely moved · **Certainty:** high
- **What passed:** the state housing tax credit (2019-20 H3998, Act 137 of 2020, 73–22 and 44–0), its cap-and-tighten revision (2021-22 H5075, Act 202 of 2022, 99–5 and 44–0), the stalled-project rescue (2023-24 S739, Act 88 of 2023, 43–0 and 84–26), tourism taxes for workforce housing (2023-24 S284, Act 57 of 2023, 34–6 and 80–29), COVID emergency rental assistance (2021-22 H3770, Act 17), and the domestic-violence lease release (2025-26 H3569, Act 184 of 2026, 108–0 and 42–4) after its predecessor (2023-24 H4158) passed the House 98–10 and died in Senate Judiciary.
- **Where the rest stopped:** rent control (three bills), inclusionary housing (three), housing-cost impact analyses (two), ADU incentives (two), church-land housing (two), the 2025-26 supply package (REIT, TIF, land designation, university housing — mostly Rep. Jones), tenant-protection and fee-disclosure bills, and the gentrification trust fund — first committee, overwhelmingly House Ways and Means (13) and House Labor, Commerce and Industry (9).
- **Examples:** 2019-20 H3998, 2023-24 S284, 2025-26 H3346 (rent control), 2025-26 H3737 (REIT).

## Home and property insurance costs

- **Bills:** 10 (all adjacent) · **Became law:** 2 · **Basket:** Got support but didn't finish · **Certainty:** medium
- **What passed:** private flood insurance (2019-20 S882, Act 166 of 2020, 46–0 and 99–0) and electronic policy delivery (2023-24 H3977, Act 108 of 2024, 43–0 and 101–0).
- **Got support but didn't finish:** the Insurance Rate Reduction and Policyholder Protection Act (2025-26 H4817) passed the House 96–17 and died in Senate Banking and Insurance; the state liquor-liability option (2023-24 H5066) passed the House 106–2 and died in the same committee.
