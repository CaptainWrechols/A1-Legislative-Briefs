# Appendix B — Theme scorecards

Per-theme deep dives behind the front brief, with each theme's history
basket and a certainty label based on how many bills support the pattern.
The baskets describe history only: "Often moved before" (similar ideas often
became law), "Got support but didn't finish" (real momentum, no enactment),
"Rarely moved before" (repeated early stops). "Adopted" counts resolutions
adopted by both chambers, which never go to the governor.

## Term limits for elected officials

- **Bills:** 37 (30 state-level measures, 5 federal-target applications, 2 rotation/court resolutions) — **Adopted: 1**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before (state level) — **certainty: high**
- Every state design died in a Judiciary committee with no hearing and no vote, 30 for 30 across four sessions: direct constitutional caps (2021-22 S302, 2023-24 H3574 and S172, 2025-26 H5360 and S590), "authorize by law" amendments (2019-20 H3023, 2025-26 H3744 — filed every session), and straight statutes (2019-20 H3024, 2025-26 H3745 and H4462).
- The exception targets Congress: 2025-26 H3008, the Article V application for a congressional term limits convention, adopted May 2025 (Senate 29–14). The broad Convention of the States application including federal term limits nearly finished twice — 2021-22 S133 passed both chambers in different forms and died when the House never took up the conference report; 2023-24 H3676 passed the House 68–30 and died on the Senate calendar.
- A chamber-rule rotation design for committee chairmen (2023-24 H4692) died in House Rules.

## Campaign money rules

- **Bills:** 36 — **Enacted: 0**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before — **certainty: high**
- One floor vote in eight years: 2023-24 H4561 (campaign funds for dependent care) passed the House 53–45 — the record's only close vote — and died in Senate Judiciary.
- Everything else died in first committee without a vote: the corporate-contribution ban (2021-22 H4877), dark-money ad disclosure filed three times including by Senate leadership (2019-20 S3, 2021-22 S174, 2025-26 S960), contributor employer disclosure (2025-26 S813), the bank-statement filing family (five bills), the pay-to-play appointment ban (four bills), public financing for Attorney General races (2021-22 H3522), and the interest-on-campaign-accounts enforcement-funding design (six bills).
- Direction check: all five contribution-limit bills would raise limits (2019-20 H4333/S800, 2021-22 H3197, 2023-24 H3474, 2025-26 H3554); none would lower them.

## Ranked choice voting and runoffs

- **Bills:** 9 — **Enacted: 0**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before — **certainty: high** (pattern), modest count
- Both directions filed, neither ever heard: allow municipal/local ranked choice voting (2021-22 H5135, 2023-24 H4022, 2025-26 H3589) and ban it statewide (2023-24 H4591, 2025-26 H3386). All five died in House Judiciary.
- The adjacent runoff-abolition bills (2023-24 H3606, 2025-26 H3552; special-primary versions H4592, H3318) also died in first committee.

## Who draws the voting maps (redistricting)

- **Bills:** 26 — **Enacted: 2** (the legislature's own maps)
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before (commission/criteria bills) — **certainty: high**
- 21 commission or criteria bills across 2019–2024 — independent commissions by constitutional amendment, citizens commissions by statute, binding-criteria acts (FAIR, Anti-Gerrymandering) — received zero hearings and zero votes, all in Judiciary committees. No commission bill was filed in 2025-26, the first session without one in this record.
- The maps themselves passed as legislature-drawn bills: 2021-22 H4493 (Act 117 of 2021: House districts; 96–14, 100–15, 43–1) and 2021-22 S865 (Act 118 of 2022: Senate and congressional districts; 41–2, 74–35, 68–36).
- In May 2026 the mid-decade congressional redraw 2025-26 H5683 passed the House 74–37 after dozens of tabled amendments (28 on roll calls), then the Senate voted 26–18 to continue (shelve) it. Companion H4717 died in committee.

## Financial disclosure and ethics enforcement

- **Bills:** 27 — **Enacted: 1**
- **Basket:** Mixed — **certainty: medium-high**
- Enacted: 2025-26 S70, the School Board Ethics Act (Act 191 of 2026; 39–2 and 109–4), after two 2019 predecessors (H3579, H4756) died in committee.
- Nearly enacted: 2025-26 H3570 (economic-interests disclosure rewrite) passed the House 102–0, passed the Senate 40–0 amended, failed House concurrence 23–86, and died in conference in the session's final days. One piece became FY 2026-27 budget proviso 117.219.
- Everything else died without votes: tax-return disclosure (three sessions), the e-filing audit department (four bills), special-purpose-district coverage (two bills), ethics-fine ballot bars (four bills), party-chairmen coverage (2025-26 S1130).

## Lobbying rules

- **Bills:** 13 — **Enacted: 0**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before — **certainty: high**
- Extending the lobbying law to local-government lobbying was filed four times, most recently by a bipartisan Senate leadership group (2019-20 H3622, 2021-22 H5194, 2025-26 S632); revolving-door extensions (2019-20 H4240, 2021-22 H4876, 2023-24 H4585) and lobbyist-report expansions (2021-22 H3920, 2023-24 H3159, 2025-26 H3475) joined them. All died in first committee.

## Open government: public records and meetings

- **Bills:** 20 — **Enacted: 0**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before — **certainty: high**
- Removing the General Assembly's own FOIA exemption (2019-20 H3259), making caucuses public bodies (H4791, H3402), a FOIA review office (H3448, H3254), a five-day response deadline (2025-26 S6), deleting the business-recruitment secrecy exemption (S396, S232), and livestreaming legislative committee meetings (2025-26 H3646) — zero votes anywhere in eight years.

## Civics and voter education

- **Bills:** 6 — **Enacted: 1**
- **Basket:** Mixed (thin) — **certainty: medium**
- The REACH Act became law on its second try: 2019-20 S35 passed the Senate 29–7 and died in House Education; 2021-22 S38 passed 45–0 and 91–12 (Act 26 of 2021).
- The K-12 design (2025-26 H3547, required middle-school civics unit) sits in House Education without a vote. Civic-education money moved through budget provisos instead (FY 2024-25 Clemson study; FY 2026-27 $2.5M USC center).
- This is the one theme whose chokepoint is House Education and Public Works rather than a Judiciary committee.

## Direct democracy: recall, initiative, referendum

- **Bills:** 3 — **Enacted: 0**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before — **certainty: medium** (small count)
- The recall amendment (2021-22 H3256) and the initiative-and-referendum amendments (2021-22 S39, 2025-26 S95) all died in Judiciary without votes. Three bills is a thin base; the pattern is consistent with the rest of the record but rests on few examples.

## The cross-theme pattern

Of the 177 core-plus-adjacent bills, 163 died in their first committee, 149
of those in House Judiciary (84) or Senate Judiciary (65); 166 of 177 never
received a passage-type floor vote at any stage; and nothing in the set ever
lost a straight passage vote on the floor. Deaths came by committee silence
or at the last step (conference, calendar, a motion to continue) — never by
a "no" vote at passage.
