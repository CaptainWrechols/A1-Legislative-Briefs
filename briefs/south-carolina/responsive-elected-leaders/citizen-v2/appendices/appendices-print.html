Appendix B — Theme scorecards

Per-theme deep dives behind the front brief, with each theme’s history basket and a certainty label based on how many bills support the pattern. The baskets describe history only: “Often moved before” (similar ideas often became law), “Got support but didn’t finish” (real momentum, no enactment), “Rarely moved before” (repeated early stops). “Adopted” counts resolutions adopted by both chambers, which never go to the governor.

Term limits for elected officials

  • Bills: 37 (30 state-level measures, 5 federal-target applications, 2 rotation/court resolutions) — Adopted: 1
  • Basket: Rarely moved before (state level) — certainty: high
  • Every state design died in a Judiciary committee with no hearing and no vote, 30 for 30 across four sessions: direct constitutional caps (2021-22 S302, 2023-24 H3574 and S172, 2025-26 H5360 and S590), “authorize by law” amendments (2019-20 H3023, 2025-26 H3744 — filed every session), and straight statutes (2019-20 H3024, 2025-26 H3745 and H4462).
  • The exception targets Congress: 2025-26 H3008, the Article V application for a congressional term limits convention, adopted May 2025 (Senate 29–14). The broad Convention of the States application including federal term limits nearly finished twice — 2021-22 S133 passed both chambers in different forms and died when the House never took up the conference report; 2023-24 H3676 passed the House 68–30 and died on the Senate calendar.
  • A chamber-rule rotation design for committee chairmen (2023-24 H4692) died in House Rules.

Campaign money rules

  • Bills: 36 — Enacted: 0
  • Basket: Rarely moved before — certainty: high
  • One floor vote in eight years: 2023-24 H4561 (campaign funds for dependent care) passed the House 53–45 — the record’s only close vote — and died in Senate Judiciary.
  • Everything else died in first committee without a vote: the corporate-contribution ban (2021-22 H4877), dark-money ad disclosure filed three times including by Senate leadership (2019-20 S3, 2021-22 S174, 2025-26 S960), contributor employer disclosure (2025-26 S813), the bank-statement filing family (five bills), the pay-to-play appointment ban (four bills), public financing for Attorney General races (2021-22 H3522), and the interest-on-campaign-accounts enforcement-funding design (six bills).
  • Direction check: all five contribution-limit bills would raise limits (2019-20 H4333/S800, 2021-22 H3197, 2023-24 H3474, 2025-26 H3554); none would lower them.

Ranked choice voting and runoffs

  • Bills: 9 — Enacted: 0
  • Basket: Rarely moved before — certainty: high (pattern), modest count
  • Both directions filed, neither ever heard: allow municipal/local ranked choice voting (2021-22 H5135, 2023-24 H4022, 2025-26 H3589) and ban it statewide (2023-24 H4591, 2025-26 H3386). All five died in House Judiciary.
  • The adjacent runoff-abolition bills (2023-24 H3606, 2025-26 H3552; special-primary versions H4592, H3318) also died in first committee.

Who draws the voting maps (redistricting)

  • Bills: 26 — Enacted: 2 (the legislature’s own maps)
  • Basket: Rarely moved before (commission/criteria bills) — certainty: high
  • 21 commission or criteria bills across 2019–2024 — independent commissions by constitutional amendment, citizens commissions by statute, binding-criteria acts (FAIR, Anti-Gerrymandering) — received zero hearings and zero votes, all in Judiciary committees. No commission bill was filed in 2025-26, the first session without one in this record.
  • The maps themselves passed as legislature-drawn bills: 2021-22 H4493 (Act 117 of 2021: House districts; 96–14, 100–15, 43–1) and 2021-22 S865 (Act 118 of 2022: Senate and congressional districts; 41–2, 74–35, 68–36).

Financial disclosure and ethics enforcement

  • Bills: 27 — Enacted: 1
  • Basket: Mixed — certainty: medium-high
  • Enacted: 2025-26 S70, the School Board Ethics Act (Act 191 of 2026; 39–2 and 109–4), after two 2019 predecessors (H3579, H4756) died in committee.

  • Bills: 20 — Enacted: 0
  • Basket: Rarely moved before — certainty: high
  • Removing the General Assembly’s own FOIA exemption (2019-20 H3259), making caucuses public bodies (H4791, H3402), a FOIA review office (H3448, H3254), a five-day response deadline (2025-26 S6), deleting the business-recruitment secrecy exemption (S396, S232), and livestreaming legislative committee meetings (2025-26 H3646) — zero votes anywhere in eight years.

Civics and voter education

  • Bills: 6 — Enacted: 1
  • Basket: Mixed (thin) — certainty: medium
  • The REACH Act became law on its second try: 2019-20 S35 passed the Senate 29–7 and died in House Education; 2021-22 S38 passed 45–0 and 91–12 (Act 26 of 2021).

Direct democracy: recall, initiative, referendum

  • Bills: 3 — Enacted: 0
  • Basket: Rarely moved before — certainty: medium (small count)
  • The recall amendment (2021-22 H3256) and the initiative-and-referendum amendments (2021-22 S39, 2025-26 S95) all died in Judiciary without votes. Three bills is a thin base; the pattern is consistent with the rest of the record but rests on few examples.

The cross-theme pattern

Of the 177 core-plus-adjacent bills, 163 died in their first committee, 149 of those in House Judiciary (84) or Senate Judiciary (65); 166 of 177 never received a passage-type floor vote at any stage; and nothing in the set ever lost a straight passage vote on the floor. Deaths came by committee silence or at the last step (conference, calendar, a motion to continue) — never by a “no” vote at passage.

Appendix F — What this data cannot say

Plain-language limits of the dataset behind the brief. None of these limits is bridged with estimates anywhere in the packet.

  1. The set is curated, not exhaustive. Bills were discovered by keyword search of official full text plus a title/summary scan of the certified statewide universe, then hand-reviewed from 5,548 hits down to 189. Four REACH Act civics bills were hand-added from the certified universe because their text contains none of the issue’s search terms — evidence that keyword discovery can miss relevant bills whose language differs. The exclusion rules are documented in the curation map.

  2. No committee vote counts exist. South Carolina publishes committee outcomes (“Favorable,” “Favorable with amendment”) but never tally tables. When this packet says a bill “died in committee,” there is no recorded vote behind that stop — usually no recorded action at all — and no committee tally may be inferred or implied.

  3. No party labels. Sponsor and vote party breakdowns require a roster join that was not fetched for this brief. The packet therefore makes no claims about which party supported or blocked anything. Phrases like “leadership of both parties” describe members’ chamber leadership roles, not a party-line vote analysis.

  4. Vote counts are verbatim and passage-type only. Floor tallies come directly from the chamber vote-history tables. Procedural motions — tabling amendments, motions to continue, rule waivers — are excluded from support signals. Two procedural votes are described in the brief because they decided outcomes, and they are labeled as what they are: the Senate’s 26–18 motion to continue H5683 (a shelving motion, not a passage vote) and the House’s 23–86 refusal to concur in Senate amendments to H3570 (a version dispute, not a vote against the House’s own 102–0 version).

  5. Resolutions are not laws. Article V applications (H3008, H3007) travel as concurrent resolutions: adopted by both chambers, never signed by the governor, and creating no state law. “Adopted” in this packet means exactly that and nothing more.

  6. “Did not pass” for the 126th session is as of the collection date (2026-08-26), after the session’s final regular calendar. Later special-session action, if any, is not in this data.

  7. The FY 2020-21 budget cycle has no enacted Part IB proviso set (the COVID continuing-resolution year), so budget-proviso coverage runs FY 2021-22 through FY 2026-27.

  8. Why bills died is not in the data. The record shows where each bill stopped, never the reasoning. Motives, deals, and informal opposition are invisible here, and the packet does not guess at them.

  9. The six constituent proposals are Forum process input from the Phase 2 Community Conversations (June–August 2026), labeled [P-…]. They record what participants proposed and how often — they are not verified facts, polling, or positions of The Forum.

[P-independent-redistricting] Independent / nonpartisan redistricting commission (anti-gerrymandering)

  • Reported frequency (process input): High — appeared in three out of four in-person events.
  • Reported consensus (process input): High — very strong support where the idea was discussed.
  • Reported concerns (process input): Some worried about how independent the commission would be. Others noted that keeping redistricting on the ten-year cycle would help.
  • Matched bills: 21 commission/criteria bills, 2019–2024 — constitutional independent commissions with referendum approval (H3044, S6, S135 in 2019; H3279, S561 in 2021; H3173 in 2023), Citizens Redistricting Commission bills (H3432, S254 in 2019; H4201, H4202 in 2021; H3243, H3245 in 2023), the Anti-Gerrymandering Act (H4222, 2023), independent-commission statutes (H3054, S230 in 2019), and the FAIR in Redistricting Act criteria bills (H4229, S750 in 2021; H3069 in 2023). All died in a Judiciary committee with no hearing and no vote.
  • Record facts on the independence concern: the filed designs answer it differently — nine members chosen by a review panel appointed by the State Inspector General with maps approved by statewide referendum; twelve members appointed by the State Ethics Commission balanced five/five/two across parties; plans protected from legislative or executive alteration or veto; or criteria-only, with the legislature keeping the pen.
  • Record facts on the ten-year-cycle concern: every filed design keys to the decennial census; the record’s one mid-decade redraw attempt (H5683, 2026) passed the House 74–37 and was shelved on the Senate floor 26–18 (motion to continue). A companion (H4717) died in committee.
  • Nearby: the legislature’s own enacted maps — H4493 (Act 117 of 2021) and S865 (Act 118 of 2022).
  • Coverage: substantial attempts, zero movement. The constitution assigns reapportionment to the General Assembly, so the full commission design requires a constitutional amendment — two-thirds of each chamber plus a referendum. No commission bill was filed in 2025-26, the first session without one in this record.

Collection and verification notes

  • Universe: every instrument of the 123rd–126th General Assemblies with full action histories, sponsor lines, latest-version texts, and all chamber roll calls; certified in sources/south-carolina/_universe/verification/universe-certification.md.

  • Curation: keep-all Pass 1 (5,548 hits) hand-reviewed to 189 bills (139 core / 38 adjacent / 12 context); exclusion rules and per-bill plain topics in working/south-carolina/responsive-elected-leaders/curation-map.json. Ambiguous bills were verified against latest-version full text before tier assignment. Four REACH Act civics bills were hand-added from the certified universe (their text contains none of the issue’s search terms) and are marked source: universe in the curation map.
  • Provisos: term-matched candidates (191–234 per cycle) hand-curated to 32 entries across six enacted cycles plus the FY 2020-21 explicit gap; every entry’s caption is checked verbatim against the source file at build time (build-proviso-curation.py).
  • Vote discipline: only passage/reading/ratification/adoption motions count as support signals; tabling, amendment, and continue motions are excluded, and the two decisive procedural votes the brief mentions are described as procedural in place.
