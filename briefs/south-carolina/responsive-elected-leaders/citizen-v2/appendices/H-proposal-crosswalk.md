# Appendix H — Citizen proposals and the record

The final Community Conversations proposal grid for this issue ("SC1 —
Responsive Elected Leaders," received 2026-09-04) carries **two proposals**
forward, with the issue framed by concern that "the state's elected leaders
fail to respond to constituent priorities, whether because of unlimited
terms or other weak accountability mechanisms." This appendix matches each
final proposal — and its reported frequency, consensus, and concerns —
against the 2019–2026 legislative record.

Provenance note: the earlier Phase 2 v2 grid listed six proposals for this
issue (adding term limits, campaign finance reform, ranked choice voting,
and financial disclosure). The final grid supersedes it; those four lanes
did not advance as proposals and now appear in this packet as the wider
accountability record (Appendices A–E carry their full bill-by-bill
detail — the record scans are unchanged). Proposal frequency, consensus,
and concerns are Forum process input, not verified facts.

## [P-independent-redistricting] Independent / nonpartisan redistricting commission (anti-gerrymandering)

- **Reported frequency (process input):** High — appeared in three out of four in-person events.
- **Reported consensus (process input):** High — very strong support where the idea was discussed.
- **Reported concerns (process input):** Some worried about how independent the commission would be. Others noted that keeping redistricting on the ten-year cycle would help.
- **Matched bills:** 21 commission/criteria bills, 2019–2024 — constitutional independent commissions with referendum approval (H3044, S6, S135 in 2019; H3279, S561 in 2021; H3173 in 2023), Citizens Redistricting Commission bills (H3432, S254 in 2019; H4201, H4202 in 2021; H3243, H3245 in 2023), the Anti-Gerrymandering Act (H4222, 2023), independent-commission statutes (H3054, S230 in 2019), and the FAIR in Redistricting Act criteria bills (H4229, S750 in 2021; H3069 in 2023). All died in a Judiciary committee with no hearing and no vote.
- **Record facts on the independence concern:** the filed designs answer it differently — nine members chosen by a review panel appointed by the State Inspector General with maps approved by statewide referendum; twelve members appointed by the State Ethics Commission balanced five/five/two across parties; plans protected from legislative or executive alteration or veto; or criteria-only, with the legislature keeping the pen.
- **Record facts on the ten-year-cycle concern:** every filed design keys to the decennial census; the record's one mid-decade redraw attempt (H5683, 2026) passed the House 74–37 and was shelved on the Senate floor 26–18 (motion to continue). A companion (H4717) died in committee.
- **Nearby:** the legislature's own enacted maps — H4493 (Act 117 of 2021) and S865 (Act 118 of 2022).
- **Coverage:** substantial attempts, zero movement. The constitution assigns reapportionment to the General Assembly, so the full commission design requires a constitutional amendment — two-thirds of each chamber plus a referendum. No commission bill was filed in 2025-26, the first session without one in this record.

## [P-voter-civics-education] Better voter education / civics and neutral civic information tools

- **Reported frequency (process input):** Medium — appeared in Charleston and Columbia.
- **Reported consensus (process input):** High — very strong support where the idea was discussed.
- **Reported concern (process input):** how neutral information would be selected and disseminated.
- **Matched bills:** the REACH Act family — S38 (Act 26 of 2021; college-level civics instruction; 45–0 and 91–12), its 2019 predecessor S35 (passed the Senate 29–7, died in House Education and Public Works), House versions H4296 (2019) and H3338 (2021) — and the middle-school civics-unit bill H3547 (2025, unheard; the State Board of Education would adopt the curriculum).
- **Record facts on the neutrality concern:** the curator question exists in the record from both directions — H3547 names the State Board of Education as curriculum curator; H4392 (2022) would have limited compelled topics and barred private funding for civics curriculum. Neither received a vote. No bill proposes a neutral voter-information tool or assigns any agency to build or curate one.
- **Nearby:** civic-education money in the budget — a Clemson civic-engagement center study (FY 2024-25 proviso 45.11) and $2.5 million for USC's Center for American Civic Leadership and Public Discourse (FY 2026-27, inside proviso 118.21).
- **Coverage:** thin with one enactment; the specific tool citizens described has no legislative history.

accountability mechanisms" as the underlying concern. For reference, the
record on those background lanes (full detail in Appendices A–E):

|---|---|---|
| Term limits | 37 (30 state-level) | None heard at the state level; the congressional term-limits Article V application (H3008) was adopted May 2025, Senate 29–14 |

| Financial disclosure & ethics enforcement | 27 | School Board Ethics Act enacted (S70, Act 191 of 2026); the H3570 disclosure rewrite passed 102–0 and 40–0 and died in conference; proviso 117.219 carried a slice |
| Lobbying rules | 13 | None received a vote |
| Open government (FOIA, meetings) | 20 | None received a vote |
| Ranked choice voting and runoffs | 9 | None received a hearing, in either direction |
| Direct democracy (recall, initiative) | 3 | None received a vote |
