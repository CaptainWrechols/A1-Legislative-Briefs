# Appendix B — Theme scorecards

Per-theme deep dives behind the front brief, with each theme's history
basket and a certainty label based on how many bills support the pattern.
The baskets describe history only: "Often moved before" (similar ideas often
became law), "Got support but didn't finish" (real momentum, no enactment),
"Rarely moved before" (repeated early stops).

## Local tools to pay for growth (penny taxes, tolls, local fees)

- **Bills:** 35 — **Enacted: 4**
- **Basket:** Mixed — **certainty: medium-high**
- The one genuinely new local tool passed: the County Green Space Sales Tax Act (2021-22 S152, Act 166 of 2022; Senate 43–1 and 41–3, House 67–28) — a voter-approved preservation penny.
- Every bill widening the *transportation* penny died in first committee without a hearing: transit-eligibility clarifications filed in all four sessions (2019-20 S611/H4389, 2021-22 H3535/S437, 2023-24 S290/S562/H4059, 2025-26 S979), penny stacking (2021-22 H3129), eleven-year reimposition (2023-24 S116), grocery exemptions (2023-24 H5188/S1113).
- All five I-95 toll bills died in first committee (2019-20 S178/H3739/S780, 2023-24 S499/S674), as did the local-option gas fee (2019-20 S172). The toll framework itself was then rewritten inside the SCDOT Modernization Act (Act 177 of 2026): usage charges only on new-capacity "choice lanes," never on existing free lanes.
- Flexibility acts passed at the edges: tourism taxes for flooding and drainage (2019-20 S217, Act 146, 34–2/87–15), beach-parking rules (2021-22 S40, Act 89, 44–0/102–10), the school-buildings penny opened to more counties (2025-26 H4589, Act 203, 81–18/40–2/91–14).

## Making developers pay (impact fees)

- **Bills:** 10 — **Enacted: 0**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before — **certainty: high**
- Ten bills over three sessions, in both directions — expanding the 1999 Development Impact Fee Act (2023-24 H4659 residential-only fees, 2025-26 H5088 resurfacing as a fundable cost, 2021-22 H3460 and 2025-26 H4008 gentrification-fund fees, 2021-22 H4943 debt payoff) and narrowing it (2023-24 H4981/S856/H5017 and 2025-26 H3165 cost exclusions, 2025-26 H4672 repeat-buyer exemption).
- Zero hearings, zero votes, either direction, eight years — Ways and Means, Finance, Judiciary, and the municipal-affairs committee.
- The one enacted impact-fee rule blocked the fees: FY 2021-22 proviso 117.96 barred impact fees on new school construction for one year and was not renewed. No proffer bill has ever been filed.

## State road money (gas tax, road fees, infrastructure funds)

- **Bills:** 26 — **Enacted: 2**
- **Basket:** Mixed — **certainty: medium-high**
- Ten measures against the gas user fee (freeze 2021-22 H4091, repeal 2021-22 H4092, suspensions 2021-22 H5103/H5112 and 2025-26 S1045/H5398/H5419/H5422/H5443/H5475) all died in the money committees without votes — as did the one bill raising the county share (2025-26 H5331) and the EV-fee repeals (2021-22 H4945, 2023-24 H3177).
- What passed tightened collection: the infrastructure maintenance fee titling fix (2021-22 H3505, Act 70, 106–4/42–2) and the EV-charging framework (2021-22 S304, Act 46, 43–1/110–2).
- The infrastructure-bank restructurings — merging its board with SCDOT's commission (2019-20 H3418) or abolishing it (2025-26 H4031) — died in Ways and Means.

## Fixing and maintaining existing roads

- **Bills:** 9 — **Enacted: 0**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before (bills) — **certainty: high**
- Every standalone maintenance-accountability bill died in first committee: the pavement preservation program (2025-26 H5363), SCDOT must maintain transferred roads (2023-24 H4610), pothole hotlines (2021-22 H3871, 2023-24 H3451), sheriff road-hazard reports (2025-26 H4687), private-road maintenance (2023-24 H3516).
- The pothole-reporting design passed inside the SCDOT Modernization Act (2025-26 S831, Act 177 of 2026): the Pothole Mitigation Program (Section 57-5-1800) — public reporting by phone, website, or a free mobile app; a seven-day repair requirement; $15,000,000 a year for full-depth repair of repeat potholes.
- The fix-first money moved through budget provisos: the yearly CTC fix-it-first rule (86.1), $417.4M in FY 2024-25, $200M in FY 2025-26, $225M in FY 2026-27, and the new Road Buyback Program (FY 2026-27 proviso 84.18). See Appendix G.

## Who runs SCDOT (governance and accountability)

- **Bills:** 10 — **Enacted: 1**
- **Basket:** Mixed — **certainty: medium-high**
- The governor-appoints-the-secretary design died unheard in 2019 (H3111) and 2024 (H5045, H4969) — then passed near-unanimously in 2026 as the SCDOT Modernization Act (S831, Act 177: Senate 37–1, House 114–0, conference report 112–2 and 43–0), with Senate leadership carrying it. The commission is abolished January 1, 2027.
- Act 177 is an omnibus, not governance alone: it also created the Pothole Mitigation Program (Section 57-5-1800), enacted phased design-build and construction manager/general contractor authority (57-5-1710/1720), rewrote toll rules around new-capacity "choice lanes," authorized public-private partnerships up to sixty years (57-3-205), required an independent external audit of SCDOT every four years, and reset the county "C"-funds split to a thirty-three percent state-highway share.
- The House's own version (2025-26 H5071) was recommitted after floor debate stalled; the dissolve-the-commission bill (2025-26 H5362) and the Lieutenant-Governor-as-secretary design (2023-24 H4629, 2025-26 H3282) died in committee.
- SCDOT expenditure-report certification (2021-22 H4090) died in Ways and Means; the project-prioritization regulation resolution (2019-20 H4369) passed the House 107–0 and was recommitted in the Senate.

## Road contracts and contractor accountability

- **Bills:** 16 — **Enacted: 3**
- **Basket:** Mixed (edges only) — **certainty: medium**
- The standalone bidding bills never reached a floor vote: design-build pilots (2023-24 H5312, 2025-26 H3560), the 2020 contractor performance-evaluation and disqualification regulation resolutions (S1069, S1070 — stranded on the Senate calendar at COVID), DBE contracting (2019-20 S385/H4401/H4823), the P3 framework (2021-22 H3559).
- The method then passed inside the SCDOT Modernization Act (2025-26 S831, Act 177 of 2026): phased design-build (57-5-1710), construction manager/general contractor authority (57-5-1720), P3 agreements capped at sixty years (57-3-205), and a required independent external audit of SCDOT every four years.
- Edges passed: utility-relocation costs on road projects (2019-20 S401, Act 36, 38–0/108–0; extended to 2032 by 2025-26 H3768, Act 244, 103–0/44–0) and the contractor-licensing rewrite (2023-24 H4115, Act 69 — licensing, not road bidding).
- The transparency tools live in provisos: published project priority lists with methodology (84.8/84.9, yearly) and the Programmed Project Viewer dashboard directive (84.18 FY 2024-25 onward). See Appendix G.

## Transit, rail, and other ways to get around

- **Bills:** 17 — **Enacted: 1**
- **Basket:** Rarely moved before — **certainty: high**
- Zero hearings on every transit bill across four sessions: complete streets (2019-20 H3656), multimodal feasibility reviews (2021-22 H3051), transit-oriented development (2019-20 H3655, 2023-24 H4013), developer transit stops (2019-20 H3828), four rail commissions/studies (2019-20 S216/H3189, 2021-22 H3937, 2025-26 H4122), the Charlotte connection study (2019-20 S730), commuter rail along highways (2023-24 H5347).
- The freight exception: the Shortline Railroad Modernization credit passed the House twice (2021-22 H4817, 106–3; 2023-24 H3737, 65–46) and died in Senate Finance both times.
- Enacted at the edges: transit-facility trespass (2025-26 S399, Act 222, 44–0/107–0).

## Planning for growth (zoning, comprehensive plans, annexation)

- **Bills:** 27 — **Enacted: 1**
- **Basket:** Mixed — **certainty: medium-high**
- No bill proposes state or regional master planning — the citizens' lever does not appear in the record (verified against the title scan and full-text discovery).
- Enacted: the Disaster Relief and Resilience Act (2019-20 S259, Act 163 of 2020, Senate 44–1, House 65–35) — the state Office of Resilience plus a required resiliency element in local comprehensive plans.
- The concurrency design got the furthest of any growth-management bill: 2025-26 S227 was reported favorably and amended on the Senate floor April 29, 2026, then died without completing passage (2023-24 H5562 and 2025-26 H4050 died in committee).
- The annexation-control bills (2023-24 H4651 county standing; 2025-26 H5742 infrastructure-study precondition; donut-hole annexation 2021-22 H5196, 2023-24 H3236, 2025-26 H4726) died in committee — and so did the developer-side bills pulling the opposite way (Home Attainability packages, permit shot clocks, TDR bills; 2025-26 S288 reached the Senate calendar).

## Water, sewer, and broadband infrastructure

- **Bills:** 14 — **Enacted: 1**
- **Basket:** Mixed — **certainty: medium**
- The Rural Infrastructure Authority consolidation passed the House 112–0 (2023-24 H3075) and died in Senate Finance; its refile (2025-26 H3373) died in Ways and Means.
- The 2019-20 broadband bills (accessibility acts S1076/S1080, broadband offices S1235 and 2021-22 S519, municipal broadband H4993, the rural study committee H4943) died in committee.
- Enacted: the Small Wireless Facilities Deployment Act (2019-20 H4262, Act 179 of 2020, 108–2/32–6/104–1) — statewide rules for 5G small cells in rights of way, limiting local zoning control.

## Related context

- **Bills:** 14 — **Enacted: 2** (both local housekeeping)
- County Transportation Committee membership bills (the county boards that spend gas-tax "C" funds) routinely pass one chamber and finish through the county's legislative delegation; two became acts (2019-20 H5030, Act 162; 2025-26 S586, Act 269). Kept for audit, excluded from headline counts.
- The road-naming process bills (2019-20 S175/S953, 2021-22 S178) — attempts to limit naming roads for living officials — died in committee; naming resolutions dominate this issue's raw search noise.
