# HB2 (budget policy trailer) — public-education sections, 2021 / 2023 / 2025

New Hampshire passes its two-year budget in two bills: HB1 (the money)
and HB2, a policy 'trailer' that bundles dozens to hundreds of legal
changes into one bill. The state's biggest education changes of this
period — the education freedom account program, the divisive-concepts
teaching ban, the $4,100 adequacy rewrite, the special-education aid
restructurings, the DEI prohibition, and the statewide cell-phone
policy mandate — were all made inside HB2, not as standalone bills.
This file is the hand-reviewed, section-level education analysis for
the three budget cycles in scope.

**Votes are on the whole trailer.** Every roll call below was cast on
HB2 as a package. A lawmaker's vote on HB2 says nothing certain about
any single section.

## HB2 2021 — Laws of 2021, Chapter 91

*468 sections extracted; 30 education-relevant after review; 42 floor roll calls on the whole bill.*

Final passage (whole bill): Senate 14–10 (Conference Committee Report, 2021-06-24); House 198–181 (Adopt Conference Committee Report, 2021-06-24).

### Core education sections

- **91:51 — Certain Differential Aid Calculations; Fiscal Year 2022.** Let districts use their higher pre-pandemic or pandemic-year enrollment count for fiscal 2022 aid, so pandemic enrollment drops would not cut adequacy grants. *(affects RSA 198:38, RSA 198:40-a, RSA 198:40-e)*
- **91:52 — Conditional Differential Aid Calculation For Pupils Eligible for a Free or Reduced Price Meal; Fiscal Year 2023.** Kept free-and-reduced-meal counts (and the aid tied to them) from collapsing in fiscal 2023 if pandemic conditions continued - a conditional hold-harmless for differentiated aid. *(affects RSA 198:38, RSA 198:40-a, RSA 198:40-e, RSA 198:41)*
- **91:53 — New Section; Relief Funding.** Created relief funding (RSA 198:40-e): an extra $600 per free-and-reduced-price-meal pupil in districts where at least 48 percent of pupils qualify, layered onto adequacy aid for the poorest districts. *(affects RSA 198)*
- **91:55 — Transfer; Education Trust Fund.** Moved $35 million of education trust fund surplus into a restricted account to pay the new relief funding in fiscal years 2022 and 2023. *(affects RSA 198:40-e)*
- **91:297 — New Subdivision; State Commission on Human Rights; Right to Freedom From Discrimination in Public Workplaces and Education.** The companion civil-rights half of the teaching ban: a new 'right to freedom from discrimination in public workplaces and education' under the human rights commission (RSA 354-A:29 and following), giving the banned-concepts rule an enforcement path including teacher-license discipline. *(affects RSA 275-E, RSA 354-A, RSA 491, RSA 98-E)*
- **91:298 — New Section; Prohibition on Teaching Discrimination.** The 'divisive concepts' teaching ban as enacted: no public school pupil shall be taught or compelled to express belief in a list of concepts about the superiority or inferiority of any group (new RSA 193:40) - the classroom-content restriction later struck down in part by a federal court and repealed in the 2025 trailer's DEI rewrite's shadow. *(affects RSA 189:39, RSA 193, RSA 354-A:34)*
- **91:311 — Community College System of New Hampshire; Dual and Concurrent Enrollment Program.** Rewrote the dual and concurrent enrollment program (RSA 188-E:25-29): high school students earn community-college credit in STEM and career courses with the state paying tuition - the main high-school-to-career bridge program. *(affects RSA 188-E:25)*
- **91:315 — New Paragraph; Kindergarten Adequate Education Grants.** Made full-day kindergarten adequacy grants a standing charge on the education trust fund every year, ending the keno-revenue-dependent formula. *(affects RSA 198:39, RSA 198:48-b)*
- **91:320 — Appropriation; Department of Education.** Appropriated $30 million from the education trust fund for school building aid on new projects - the first new-project money after a decade-long moratorium era. *(affects RSA 198:15-a)*
- **91:322 — Education Tax Revenue; Fiscal Year 2023.** Cut the statewide education property tax (SWEPT) for fiscal year 2023: the rate is set to raise $263 million instead of the usual $363 million target - a one-year, $100 million cut in the local school tax the state counts as its aid. *(affects RSA 76:3, RSA 76:8, RSA 82, RSA 83-F)* *(hand-added; missed by term matching)*
- **91:323 — Supplemental Education Payment; Fiscal Year 2023.** Guaranteed no municipality's total education grant would fall because of the SWEPT cut: the state makes up any decrease with a supplemental payment. *(affects RSA 198:41, RSA 198:42)*
- **91:431 — New Chapter; Education Freedom Accounts.** Created the education freedom account (EFA) program (new RSA 194-F): state adequacy money follows a student out of public school to private, religious, or home education expenses, paid from the education trust fund through a scholarship organization - the biggest school-choice change of the period. *(affects RSA 193-A:5, RSA 193-C:6, RSA 193:1, RSA 194-F)*
- **91:432 — Duty of Parent; Compulsory Attendance by Pupil.** Amended the compulsory-attendance statute so participation in the EFA program satisfies a child's school-attendance duty - the conforming change that makes the program legal schooling. *(affects RSA 193:1, RSA 194-F, RSA 21-N:11)*

### Adjacent (administration and funding-mechanics) sections

- **91:54 — Additional Aid to Education.** Let a school district call a special meeting to decide how to budget any adjustment in its education aid. *(affects RSA 197:3-a)*

- **91:96 — Education Tax Credit Scholarship Organizations; 2027.** Conforming change to the education tax credit scholarship statute for the interest-and-dividends repeal: individuals' I&D-tax credits for scholarship-organization donations end with the tax in 2027 (later 2025). *(affects RSA 77, RSA 77-A, RSA 77-A:5, RSA 77-E)*

- **91:314 — New Paragraph; Ten Year Plan for Grant Projects.** Required the department of education to maintain a 10-year school facilities plan ranking potential school building aid projects. *(affects RSA 198:15-a, RSA 198:15-c, RSA 541-A)*
- **91:316 — Appropriation; Department of Education.** Appropriated $1.9 million to top up fiscal 2021 kindergarten and adequacy grant payments. *(affects RSA 198:48-b)*
- **91:318 — School Planning Committees; Vacancies.** Rewrote how vacancies on cooperative and area school planning committees and school boards are filled - district governance mechanics. *(affects RSA 671:33)*
- **91:398 — System of Care for Children's Mental Health; Duties of the Commissioner of the Department of Education; Reference Change.** Folded the department of education into the children's mental health system of care plan - schools as a delivery point for children's behavioral health. *(affects RSA 135-F:5)*
- **91:425 — Medicaid to Schools Program; Fiscal Committee Approval of Supplemental Funding.** Let the Medicaid-to-schools program accept extra federal money mid-biennium with fiscal committee approval - the funding stream for school-based health services.

## HB2 2023 — Laws of 2023, Chapter 79

*612 sections extracted; 43 education-relevant after review; 17 floor roll calls on the whole bill.*

Final passage (whole bill): House 326–53 (Concur (with Senate changes), 2023-06-08).

### Core education sections

- **79:3 — Career and Technical Education; Dual and Concurrent Enrollment Program.** Extended the dual and concurrent enrollment program to career and technical education center students in grades 10 through 12. *(affects RSA 188-)*
- **79:4 — Regional Career and Technical Education; Dual and Concurrent Enrollment Program.** Doubled the funded dual/concurrent enrollment courses per student (from 2 to 4 in each of grades 10, 11, and 12). *(affects RSA 188-E:27)*
- **79:62 — New Section; Department of Education; The Commission on New Hampshire Civics; Commission Established.** Created the commission on New Hampshire civics inside the department of education - the body charged with building the state's civics-education program. *(affects RSA 14:49, RSA 21-N)*
- **79:65 — New Section; Department of Education; Higher Education; Recruitment of Educators.** Created an educator recruitment program: stipends and grants for academic residencies and alternative certification pathways, aimed at the teacher shortage. *(affects RSA 21-N)*
- **79:80 — New Chapter; Computer Science Educator Program.** Created the computer science educator program (new RSA 200-O): tuition-reimbursement incentives for teachers earning computer science credentials - the trailer's main classroom-technology investment. *(affects RSA 21-N:13, RSA 541-A, RSA by)*
- **79:141 — Special Education; State Aid.** Changed special education state aid so districts are reimbursed for costs above 3.5 times the state average per-pupil cost - the 'catastrophic aid' threshold that decides when the state steps in. *(affects RSA 186-C:18, RSA 186-C:19-b)*
- **79:143 — Liability for Children with Disabilities in Certain Court Ordered Placements.** Shifted liability for certain court-ordered placements of children with disabilities, defining when the state rather than the school district pays. *(affects RSA 169-B, RSA 169-B:13, RSA 169-B:40, RSA 186-)*
- **79:150 — Cost of an Opportunity for an Adequate Education.** Rewrote the cost of an adequate education (RSA 198:40-a): base per-pupil aid rises to $4,100 with larger add-ons for free-and-reduced-lunch pupils, special education, and English learners - the formula rewrite the whole 2023 education budget turned on. *(affects RSA 193-C:3, RSA 193-C:6, RSA 193-E:2-a, RSA 198:40-a)*

- **79:154 — Extraordinary Need Grants; 2025.** Set the 2025-and-after version of extraordinary need grants, extending the property-poor-town aid formula. *(affects RSA 198:40-a, RSA 198:40-f)*
- **79:156 — Determination of Education Grants.** Rewrote how each municipality's total education grant is determined, including hold-harmless rules so no town's grant falls below prior levels while the new formula phases in. *(affects RSA 198:40-a, RSA 198:40-e, RSA 198:40-f, RSA 198:41)*
- **79:157 — Chartered Public Schools; Funding.** Raised chartered public school per-pupil funding: adequacy amounts plus an additional grant, with the amount indexed going forward - the charter funding statute's biggest rewrite of the period. *(affects RSA 194-B:11, RSA 194-B:3-a, RSA 198:40-a, RSA 198:40-d)*
- **79:158 — Repeal; Third Grade Reading Accountability.** Repealed the third-grade reading accountability data requirement from the statewide assessment statute (RSA 193-C) - a piece of the testing regime removed. *(affects RSA 193-C:3)*
- **79:191 — Business Profits Tax; Distribution to Education Trust Fund.** Fixed the education trust fund's share of the business profits tax at 41 percent of revenue - replacing the old 'increase attributable to' formula with a straight statutory split. *(affects RSA 198:39, RSA 77-A:2, RSA 77-A:20-a)*
- **79:192 — Business Enterprise Tax; Distribution to Education Trust Fund.** Fixed the education trust fund's share of the business enterprise tax at 41 percent of revenue, the same straight split. *(affects RSA 198:39, RSA 77-E:14, RSA 77-E:2)*
- **79:605 — Career and Technical Education Incentive Grant.** Created the career and technical education incentive grant to pay transportation costs that keep sending-district students out of regional CTE centers.

## HB2 2025 — Laws of 2025, Chapter 141

*461 sections extracted; 32 education-relevant after review; 45 floor roll calls on the whole bill.*

Final passage (whole bill): Senate 16–8 (Conference Committee Report, 2025-06-26); House 184–183 (Adopt Conference Committee Report, 2025-06-26).

### Core education sections

- **141:16 — Education Freedom Accounts; Contingency.** Made the education freedom account expansion's effective date contingent on program participation certification - the mechanics attached to 2025's removal of the EFA income cap (2025, 75) that opened the program to every family. *(affects RSA 194-F:3)*
- **141:80 — Education; School Money; Education Trust Fund Created and Invested.** Capped the education trust fund's balance: any surplus over $20 million at the close of a biennium is swept into the general fund - money that once stayed dedicated to schools. *(affects RSA 198:39, RSA 21-I:8)*
- **141:132 — Business Profits Tax; Distribution of Funds.** Cut the education trust fund's share of the business profits tax from 41 percent to 39 percent, keeping more business tax revenue in the general fund. *(affects RSA 198:39, RSA 77-A:2, RSA 77-A:20-a)*
- **141:133 — Business Enterprise Tax; Distribution of Funds.** Cut the education trust fund's share of the business enterprise tax from 41 percent to 39 percent. *(affects RSA 198:39, RSA 77-E:14, RSA 77-E:2)*
- **141:223 — Education; Adequate Education; Education Trust Fund; Annual Adjustment.** Delayed the annual 2 percent inflation adjustment of per-pupil adequacy aid to July 2026 - a pause in the automatic growth of state school aid. *(affects RSA 194-B:11, RSA 198:40-a, RSA 198:40-d, RSA 198:40-f)*
- **141:224 — Education; Adequate Education; Education Trust Fund; Extraordinary Need Grants.** Re-enacted the extraordinary need grant formula - the extra aid tied to a town's equalized property value per pupil. *(affects RSA 198:40-a, RSA 198:40-f)*
- **141:225 — New Section; Education; Adequate Education; Education Trust Fund; Fiscal Capacity Disparity Aid.** Created fiscal capacity disparity aid: up to $1,250 extra per pupil for municipalities with less than $1 million of equalized property value per pupil, phasing out by $1.6 million - aid aimed squarely at property-poor towns. *(affects RSA 198, RSA 198:40-a)*
- **141:226 — Education; Adequate Education; Education Trust Fund; Determination of Education Grants.** Rewrote how each municipality's education grant is computed to fold in the new fiscal capacity disparity aid. *(affects RSA 198:40-a, RSA 198:40-f, RSA 198:40-g, RSA 198:41)*
- **141:228 — Instruction in National and State History, Government, and Civics.** Rewrote the required civics instruction statute (RSA 189:11): the locally developed civics competency assessment and the naturalization-test requirement for graduation - the testing piece of the civics program. *(affects RSA 189:11)*
- **141:275 — Education; Special Education; Program Approval, Monitoring, and Corrective Action.** Ordered an independent, nationally recognized audit of the state's special education program approval and monitoring system - the oversight response to exploding special-education costs. *(affects RSA 186-C:3-b, RSA 186-C:5)*
- **141:276 — Education; Special Education; State Aid.** Restructured special education state aid administration and reimbursement - the catastrophic-cost aid that shields local budgets. *(affects RSA 186-C:18, RSA 186-C:19-b)*
- **141:278 — Education; Special Education; State Aid.** Rewrote the funding rule for statewide special education programs. *(affects RSA 186-C:18, RSA 198:39)*
- **141:321 — New Subdivision; Prohibition on Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion.** The statewide companion: the same DEI prohibition applied to every state agency and political subdivision (new RSA 21-I:112 and following), which reaches school districts as public employers. *(affects RSA 21-I, RSA 354-A:1)*
- **141:322 — New Subdivision; Prohibition on Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion in Public Schools.** Prohibited diversity, equity, and inclusion programs in public schools (new RSA 186:71 and following): no DEI offices, officers, trainings, or DEI-based hiring or contracting in schools, with enforcement through the department of education - the 2025 trailer's biggest education-policy section. *(affects RSA 186, RSA 186:71, RSA 354-A:1)*
- **141:389 — Legislative Declaration of Authority Regarding Public Education.** Declared the legislature's position on the Claremont school-funding rulings - asserting that defining and funding an adequate education is the legislature's prerogative - while the ConVal litigation over adequacy amounts and the SWEPT remains in the courts.
- **141:455 — New Paragraph; Cell Phone Use Policy.** Required every school board and charter school to adopt a policy governing student cell phones and other personal electronic devices in school - the statewide 'bell-to-bell' phone-policy mandate, with exceptions for IEPs, 504 plans, and medical needs. *(affects RSA 189:1-a)*
