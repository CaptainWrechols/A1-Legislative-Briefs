# Reality map — Housing Affordability in New Hampshire

Internal analysis for the citizen brief. All claims fact-checked against
evidence-pack.json by fact-check-reality-map.py.

**Coverage caveat.** 2020-2024 covers only bills that received a floor roll call (plus HB2 budget trailers); committee- and voice-vote deaths in those years are not in the set. 2025-2026 coverage is complete from the official database. Cross-year failure-rate comparisons are unsafe; within-year and topic patterns are reliable.

### Building more homes: zoning and land use — *Often moved before*

Every enactment is from 2025-2026: homes in commercial zones (HB631 2025, HB1010 2026, HB1588 2026), limits on local dwelling-unit restrictions (HB457 2025), plus the 2021 planning omnibus SB86. The repeal attempts against these new laws (HB1011, HB1012, HB1016 in 2026) all died.

Examples: 2025:HB631, 2026:HB1010, 2026:HB1588, 2024:SB538, 2026:SB90

### Accessory dwelling units (ADUs) — *Got support but didn't finish*

The sweeping version (two ADUs per lot by right, HB1291 2024) passed the House 220-143 and was killed in the Senate. Narrower wins followed: HB577 (2025) broadened the ADU definition including detached ADUs, and HB1079 (2026) allowed ADUs in non-conforming structures. ADU finance bills (HB604, HB1662) died.

Examples: 2024:HB1291, 2025:HB577, 2026:HB1079, 2026:HB604

### State housing money and programs — *Mixed*

Standalone money bills usually die - often after unanimous Senate votes - while the same dollars move through HB2, the budget trailer: Affordable Housing Fund ($25M in 2021 and again in 2023), InvestNH, Housing Champion ($5M), Partners in Housing. New taxes for housing (HB1786, HB1707) and transfer-tax increases (HB530, SB81) never reached a floor win.

Examples: 2021:SB152, 2023:SB145, 2026:HB572, 2026:HB1786, 2026:HB1042

### Renting: costs, fees, and rules — *Rarely moved before*

Application-fee limits, rent registries, algorithm bans, pet-fee rules, mold remediation, and municipal rental regulation all died, most in the House. What passed is narrow: optional electronic rent payment (HB309 2025) and an EV-charging study for renters (HB111 2023).

Examples: 2023:HB95, 2025:HB558, 2026:HB1371, 2026:HB1612, 2025:HB309

### Eviction and tenant protections — *Rarely moved before*

Voucher-discrimination bans died three times (2022, 2023, 2025). Notice-before-sale died three times (2023, 2025, and the 2022 voucher companion). What passed is procedural: the landlord-tenant court omnibus (SB126 2021) and eviction notice/process updates (HB1598 2026). The 2020 COVID eviction pause was vetoed; the override failed.

Examples: 2022:HB1291, 2023:HB469, 2025:HB628, 2021:SB126, 2026:HB1598, 2020:HB1247

### Homelessness and housing stability — *Mixed*

Standalone shelter-funding bills died at Senate deadlines or on tables (SB415 2022, SB113/SB114 2025, HB519); the big shelter dollars moved through HB2 ($10M in 2023; $10M opioid-fund shelter money in 2025). The Council on Housing Stability was codified (HB503, enacted 2022) and updated (SB179 2025).

Examples: 2022:SB415, 2025:SB113, 2021:HB503, 2025:SB179

### Manufactured, tiny, and factory-built homes — *Mixed*

Resident protections advanced narrowly (SB166 2025 sale notice) and tiny houses/yurts were legalized as innovative housing (HB1681 2026). But the dispute-resolution Board of Manufactured Housing was repealed by HB2 2023 while the bill to fix it (SB203) died between the chambers, and by-right manufactured housing (HB685 2025) died in conference.

Examples: 2025:SB166, 2026:HB1681, 2023:SB203, 2025:HB685

### Land use boards, appeals, and the courts — *Mixed*

Conflict-of-interest recusal (HB92 2025) and abutter-appeal rules (HB1359 2024) became law; board training died repeatedly (2020 HB1629, 2021 HB586, 2022 SB400, 2026 HB1802). The Housing Appeals Board was restructured through HB2 2025, not a standalone bill.

Examples: 2025:HB92, 2024:HB1359, 2020:HB1629, 2026:HB1802

### Housing studies, plans, and data — *Often moved before*

Study vehicles pass more easily (zoning enabling act commission HB399 2025, housing investment trust study HB633 2025), and the Senate routed several 2026 housing bills to interim study. A 10-year strategic housing plan (HB1814) stayed on the House table.

Examples: 2025:HB399, 2025:HB633, 2026:HB1814

## Topic reality cards

### Letting homes be built in commercial zones

This is the record's clearest recent success. HB631 (2025) permitted residential building in commercial zoning - the House rejected a motion to kill it 128-211 - and became law. HB1010 (2026) extended multi-family residential development on commercially zoned land and became law (Chapter 319). HB1588 (2026) packaged multifamily-in-commercial-districts with parking-mandate limits and housing infrastructure grant money and passed a conference report 185-171. Broader versions (HB1065, SB90 - high-density residential on commercial land) still died.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** None for the moderate versions since 2025; the aggressive density versions die on the House or Senate floor.
- **Who carried them:** Cross-party House coalitions; 78 House Republicans joined most Democrats to keep HB631 alive.
- **Venue:** State legislature (statewide zoning preemption).
- **What a group would still need to learn:** How many homes the new commercial-zone laws actually produce (first data due via municipal reporting) / Whether 2027 sees repeal attempts like 2026's HB1011/HB1016

### Expanding accessory dwelling units (ADUs)

The big statewide mandate - two ADUs per lot by right (HB1291, 2024) - passed the House 220-143 (150 of 181 Democrats and 67 Republicans voting yes) and was killed in the Senate, which indefinitely postponed it after a rescue amendment failed 8-16. The next two years produced narrower wins: HB577 (2025) broadened the ADU definition including detached units and became law (Chapter 197); HB1079 (2026) let ADUs go into non-conforming structures (Chapter 210). A 2026 attempt to repeal the ADU statutes outright (HB1012) died, ADU loan guarantees (HB1662) and ADU parking relief (HB1006) also died, while ADU loan forgiveness (HB604) went to interim study.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** The Senate floor for broad mandates; House committees for finance add-ons.
- **Who carried them:** Cross-party House majorities; Senate Democrats offered the failed 2024 rescue amendment.
- **Venue:** State legislature sets the statewide ADU floor (RSA 674); towns regulate above it.
- **What a group would still need to learn:** Whether ADU production data (HB1625's failed reporting idea) emerges another way / Whether a Senate-palatable version of the two-ADU mandate exists

### Streamlining local zoning procedures for housing

The same idea has now failed three ways in three years: SB538 (2024) passed the Senate and was killed on the House floor 188-173; SB84 (2025-2026) passed the Senate 13-10, was carried across the biennium, and ended in interim study; the House's own vehicles (HB1008, HB1713) died in committee or on the floor in 2026. What has passed instead is targeted: HB457 (2025) limiting specific local dwelling-unit restrictions.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** The House floor and House Housing committee.
- **Who carried them:** Senate Republicans (Sen. Keith Murphy prime-sponsored SB84) with split House votes.
- **Venue:** State legislature (RSA 674-676 preemption of local process).
- **What a group would still need to learn:** What the interim study on SB84 recommends for 2027 / Whether procedure bills fare differently if split into single-subject pieces

### Putting state money into the Affordable Housing Fund

The dollars are real but they never arrive by standalone bill. SB152 (2021) passed the Senate 24-0 and the House killed it - while the 2021 budget trailer (HB2, 91:376) carried $25 million for the fund. HB2 2023 (79:43) carried another $25 million. The standalone attempts to make funding automatic - raising the real estate transfer tax share (HB530, SB81 2025) - died at Senate deadlines, and SB419 (2026, Housing Champion plus fund appropriations) went to interim study 16-8. New dedicated taxes for housing (HB1786 luxury second homes; HB1707 vacancy tax) died on or before the House floor.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** Standalone: the second chamber or the Senate deadline. Via HB2: none - it has passed every cycle.
- **Who carried them:** Senate sponsors of both parties (SB152 was Sen. Gray, R); budget writers deliver the money.
- **Venue:** State legislature, in practice through the biennial budget.
- **What a group would still need to learn:** Whether fund appropriations continue in the 2027 budget / What happens to InvestNH/Housing Champion balances as federal money winds down

### Rewarding towns that welcome housing (Housing Champion / InvestNH / Partners in Housing)

The incentive-program family is the record's steadiest thread - and it lives in HB2. SB145 (2023) passed the Senate 21-3 and its Housing Champion program was enacted through HB2 2023 (79:462-466, $5M). HB2 2025 extended the money (141:355) and added Partners in Housing for workforce housing on public land (141:212) - after the standalone version (HB572) had been killed on the House floor 191-156. A 2026 repeal of Housing Champion (HB1196) passed the House 185-166 on near-party lines (Republicans yes) and was killed by the Senate. The business-loan variant (SB279 2025) died at the Senate deadline.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** Standalone bills die in the House; the programs survive inside the budget and survived a repeal attempt.
- **Who carried them:** Senate sponsors across parties (SB145: Sen. Perkins Kwoka, D); House Republicans led the repeal.
- **Venue:** State legislature via the budget; municipalities opt in voluntarily.
- **What a group would still need to learn:** Whether repeal returns in 2027 / How many municipalities win designation and build

### Banning discrimination against tenants with housing vouchers

Filed three times, dead three times, each earlier than the last: 2022's HB1291 was tabled on the House floor 179-148 after an 11-10 committee split; 2023's HB469 was killed by voice vote after the tabling pattern; 2025's HB628 died in the House. No version has ever passed either chamber.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** The House floor.
- **Who carried them:** House Democrats.
- **Venue:** State legislature (RSA 354-A / RSA 540 amendments).
- **What a group would still need to learn:** Whether a narrower version (e.g. veterans' vouchers) changes the vote / What share of NH landlords accept vouchers today

### Regulating rents, fees, and rental practices

Nothing that touches the price of renting has passed. Municipal rental regulation authority (HB95 2023) was crushed 301-63. Rent-increase notice (HB567 2023) lost on the House floor. The rent registry + algorithm ban (HB558 2025), the application-fee ban (HB1371 2026), the one-fee-per-year cap (HB1375 2026), the price-fixing-software ban (HB1612 2026), pet-fee rules (HB1553 2026), and rental mold remediation (HB1143 2026) all died in the House. The exceptions run the other way or are narrow: HB309 (2025) made electronic rent payment optional, and HB1336 (2026) - loosening the security-deposit cap - passed both chambers and was vetoed.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** The House, usually its Housing or Judiciary committees and the floor.
- **Who carried them:** House Democrats on tenant-cost bills; Republicans on the deposit-cap exception.
- **Venue:** State legislature; NH has no local rent regulation authority to begin with.
- **What a group would still need to learn:** Whether the HB1336 veto is overridden this fall / Whether fee-transparency (rather than fee-limit) framing changes outcomes

### Eviction rules and tenant stability

Procedural updates pass; substantive protections do not. SB126 (2021), an omnibus rewrite of landlord-tenant proceedings, passed the Senate 24-0 and became law (Chapter 152); HB1598 (2026) updated eviction notice and court process (Chapter 308). But 60-day notice for at-will evictions (HB351 2025), renovation-eviction limits (HB401 2023, lost 154-213), notice before a building is sold (HB112 2023, HB444 2025), and Social Security disruption safeguards (HB1171 2026, killed 186-155) all died in the House. The landlord-side expansion of eviction grounds (HB1499 2026) passed the House 193-154 and died in conference. The 2020 COVID eviction/foreclosure pause (HB1247) passed both chambers and was vetoed; the override failed 187-148.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** The House floor for tenant-side bills; conference for landlord-side.
- **Who carried them:** Democrats on tenant-side bills; Republicans on HB1499.
- **Venue:** State legislature (RSA 540).
- **What a group would still need to learn:** Whether notice-before-sale returns for a fourth try / How the new HB1598 process changes eviction case outcomes

### Funding homeless shelters and services

Standalone shelter funding has never passed in this record: SB415 (2022, shelter rates) was tabled 13-11 in the Senate; SB113 and SB114 (2025) passed unanimous or near-unanimous Senate votes and then died at the Senate's own end-of-year deadline; HB519 (Waypoint youth shelter) died in the House. The money that did arrive came through HB2: $8M for shelter rates plus $2M for cold-weather capacity in 2023, and $5M a year of opioid-settlement money for year-round emergency shelter in 2025. The Council on Housing Stability was codified by HB503 (enacted 2022) and updated by SB179 (2025).

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** Standalone: Senate Finance and the calendar. Via HB2: none.
- **Who carried them:** Senate Democrats (SB415: Sen. Kahn) and bipartisan sponsors on councils.
- **Venue:** State legislature via the budget; DHHS administers.
- **What a group would still need to learn:** What happens when opioid-fund shelter money ends in 2027 / Whether shelter rates keep pace with reported per-bed costs

### Manufactured homes, tiny homes, and their residents

Two narrow wins: SB166 (2025) requires notice before a manufactured home in a resident-owned community is sold, and HB1681 (2026) legalized tiny houses and yurts as innovative housing (House concurrence 242-102). The losses are structural: HB2 2023 (79:301) repealed the Board of Manufactured Housing - the dispute forum for park residents - while SB203, which would have restructured it, died between the chambers; SB318 (2024) on the installation-standards board was vetoed; by-right manufactured housing in residential zones (HB685 2025) died in conference; and 2026 bills to weaken manufactured-housing requirements (HB1016) also died.

- **Where similar ideas stopped:** Conference committees and the governor; the board repeal happened inside the budget trailer.
- **Who carried them:** Mixed sponsors; resident-protection bills largely from Democrats, deregulation from Republicans.
- **Venue:** State legislature (RSA 205-A); the board's functions now sit with the courts/OPLC.
- **What a group would still need to learn:** Where park residents take disputes now that the board is gone / Whether by-right manufactured housing returns

### Chokepoints

- **House floor (Inexpedient to Legislate)** — the single largest killer in the set; includes 301-63 (HB95 2023) and dozens of 2026 kills (certainty: high)
- **Senate Rule 3-23 end-of-year deadline** — five 2025 Senate housing bills (SB113, SB114, SB279, SB81, SB86) died at the 10/31/2025 deadline after passing or awaiting action (certainty: high)
- **Committees of conference** — HB685 (2025), HB1499 and HB1540 (2026), HB1662 (2022) died without agreed reports (certainty: medium)
- **The governor's desk** — three vetoes in the set: HB1247 (2020, COVID protections), SB318 (2024), HB1336 (2026, deposit-cap exception, override pending as of Aug 2026) (certainty: high)
- **The budget trailer (as a bypass)** — Housing Champion, Partners in Housing, Affordable Housing Fund money, shelter funding, and the Housing Appeals Board restructure all moved through HB2 rather than standalone bills (certainty: high)

### Party patterns (cited votes only)

- The 2021 zoning omnibus SB86 passed the House 208-167 with 196 Republicans in favor and 164 Democrats opposed.
- The 2024 ADU mandate HB1291 passed the House 220-143 with 150 Democrats and 67 Republicans in favor.
- The 2026 Housing Champion repeal HB1196 passed the House 185-166 with Republicans in favor and every voting Democrat opposed; the Senate then killed the repeal.
- Supply-side zoning coalitions are cross-party and shift bill by bill; tenant-cost bills split on party lines.

## Deliberation prompts

1. Standalone housing-money bills die while the same dollars pass inside HB2. If an idea needs state money, what does that pattern suggest about timing it to a budget year?
2. The two-ADU mandate passed the House with 220 votes and died in the Senate. Is the obstacle the idea, or its scope - and what did the narrower ADU laws that passed leave out?
3. Tenant-cost bills (fees, notice, vouchers) have stopped on the House floor in every recent session. What would have to change for one to reach the Senate?
4. The Legislature keeps choosing incentives for willing towns (Housing Champion, Partners in Housing) over mandates on all towns. Which trade-offs matter most to your group?
5. Five 2025 Senate housing bills died at the Senate's own end-of-year deadline, several after unanimous votes. Is that a 'no' on the ideas, or a calendar problem?
6. The Board of Manufactured Housing was repealed inside a 600-section budget bill while the standalone fix died. How should groups track housing policy that moves inside HB2?
7. Three housing vetoes in six years, with one override pending. When a proposal passes both chambers on party-line votes, what does the record say happens next?
8. The 2026 session killed 51 housing bills and sent 11 to interim study. Which of those studies are worth following into 2027?
