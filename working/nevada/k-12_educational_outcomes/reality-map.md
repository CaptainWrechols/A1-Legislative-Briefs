## The headline

Education is the Legislature's biggest subject and its most crowded funnel:
513 policy bills in four sessions, 228 became law, and **193 of the 269
failures never left their first committee** — 127 of them dying inside an
Education committee. The funding-formula fight citizens remember was settled
in 2019 (SB543's Pupil-Centered Funding Plan). What stalls now is oversight
machinery (seven independent-auditor bills dead in committee), classroom-time
mandates (the 2025 recess bill died without a vote), and measurement changes
(the growth-weighting bill died in committee; social-emotional measurement
died on the Senate floor twice). Since 2023 a veto wall has appeared: **13
education vetoes in 2023–2025**, concentrated on bargaining, school-adjacent
health governance, and library bills — while raises, audits, and funding acts
were signed.

## Statement reality cards (ten)

Full structured cards live in `reality-map.json → proposal_reality_cards`. Summary:

1. **Audit spending / classroom dollars** — *tried seven times, never voted on.* The independent inspector-general design died in its first committee in every session: 2019 AB146/AB296, 2021 AB108, 2023 AB149 (an Inspector General of Education) and AB353, 2025 AB33/AB154. Asm. Brittney Miller (D) carried it four straight sessions; a Republican version (AB353) died the same way. What passed keeps auditing inside the Legislature: AB517 (2023), recurring performance audits of the two biggest districts and the charter authority, 42-0 and 21-0. Existing law also already requires six-year outside-consultant financial reviews — a 2025 bill to repeal them (SB411) failed too.
2. **Per-pupil funding** — *the machinery exists; the guarantee keeps dying.* SB543 (2019) built the Pupil-Centered Funding Plan (18-3, 34-7); SB439 (2021) rebuilt its revenues; AB495 (2021) added the mining tax (28-14, 16-5); SB503 (2023) and SB500 (2025, 42-0/13-8) are the biennium acts; SB231 (2023) funded educator raises (21-0, 41-1). Bills locking in a floor — AB459 (2023, budget must keep pace with inflation-plus-enrollment) and SB471 (2025) — died in the money committees. No bill sets a national-average target.
3. **Recess / instructional minutes** — *reached the Legislature once, no vote.* AB53 (2025): 20 minutes of daily outdoor recess K-5 and a ban on withholding recess for academics or behavior — died in Assembly Education. The PE-content bill (SB182, 2021) and the fourth social-studies credit (AB228, 2023) also died. Contrast: content mandates the leadership wanted — financial literacy (AB274, 2023, 40-0/20-0) and cellphone policies (SB444, 2025, 42-0/21-0) — passed nearly unanimously.
4. **Evidence-based ed-tech** — *never filed.* No bill requires independent proof before districts buy instructional technology, and none limits screen time for young pupils. The nearest laws are guardrails at the edges: pupil-data disclosure (SB403, 2019), home-device tracking (SB66, 2021), cellphone policies (SB444, 2025), and a ban on AI doing counselors' work (AB406, 2025, 42-0/20-1). The state's ed-tech commission was abolished in 2023 (SB214).
5. **Universal pre-K** — *investment precedent, no entitlement bill.* SB84 (2019) put the state pre-K grant program in statute (41-0, 20-0); the literacy-and-readiness account (AB400, 2023) and family-engagement programs (AB348 2023, AB212 2025) followed. Structure and expansion keep dying: statutory early-childhood offices failed in 2023 (AB113, Assembly floor) and 2025 (SB82, committee); the eligibility expansion tied to per-pupil funding (SB58, 2025) died in Senate Finance.
6. **Administrator/superintendent accountability** — *principals: done twice; boards: a decade of committee deaths; superintendent pay: untouched.* SB292 (2023) made principals at-will when ratings fall and half the teachers ask out (21-0, 39-3); SB460 (2025) added elementary achievement plans with principal consequences and a Public Education Oversight Board (21-0, 38-4). Appointed-school-board bills died in committee in 2019, 2021 and 2023 before AB175 (2023) seated four appointed nonvoting Clark County trustees (29-11, 16-4); the 2025 upgrade (AB156) passed the Assembly 24-18 and died in Senate Education. No bill touches superintendent pay or principal pay-by-enrollment.
7. **Teacher pay and the shortage** — *the raise happened; the floors and subsidies didn't.* SB231 (2023) funded raises for districts that budget them; AB398 (2025) funds hard-to-fill differentials (20-0, 41-1). The pipeline passed nearly unanimously: the Interstate Teacher Mobility Compact (SB442), pathway grants (AB428, AB515), one-year provisional out-of-state hires (AB49, 2025). Dead: the $45,000 minimum salary (SB445, 2019, R-sponsored, committee), the grade-3 class-size cap (AB335, 2023), and — at the governor's desk — the substitute insurance subsidy (AB282, 31-11/16-4, vetoed) and class-size bargaining (AB155, 2025, vetoed).
8. **Growth-based / broader assessment** — *the delta bill died without a vote.* SB351 (2025), double-weighting growth of the lowest-scoring pupils in star ratings, died in Senate Education. Social-emotional measurement (SB313 2023, SB314 2025 — Lange (D), with Krasner (R) in 2023) cleared committee and died on the Senate floor both times. Competency-based expansion passed the Senate 21-0 and died in the second house (SB403, 2025). What passed trims tests: the test-time cap (SB353, 2021, 42-0/21-0) and the end of end-of-course finals (SB9, 2023).
9. **Read by Grade 3** — *retention went the other way; the assessment fix died at the finish line.* AB289 (2019) removed automatic retention (28-11, 17-4) — no bill since proposes reinstating it. No bill repeals the assessment; the nearest, AB386 (2025, Washoe alternative-assessment pilot), passed 40-0 and 21-0 and died awaiting an Assembly concurrence vote when the session ended. SB278 (2025) made the statewide assessment the sole deficiency trigger (42-0, 21-0). The phonics-side bill (AB187, 2023, three-cueing ban) died in committee. The 2020 special session cut the program's $31.4M year-two transfer and made it a restoration priority.
10. **School mental health** — *framework in law, mandates stall, governance vetoed.* SB319 (2019) defined counselor/psychologist/social-worker roles with full-time counselors "to the extent money is available" (41-0, 21-0); SB151 (2021) required urban ratio-improvement plans; SB277 (2025) added social workers and a licensure pipeline (38-1, 15-5). The unconditional counselor mandate (AB298, 2025) and monthly workshops (AB374, 2025) died in committee. The children's mental-health consortium passed 42-0 and 20-0 and was vetoed (AB265, 2023).

## People and process signals (facts only)

- 340 of 513 policy bills came from named lawmakers; 173 from committees.
- Most frequent primaries (name variants merged): Sen. Marilyn Dondero Loop (D, 37), Sen. Scott Hammond (R, 31), Sen. Carrie Buck (R, 31), Asm. Selena Torres(-Fossett) (D, 27), Asm. Brittney Miller (D, 24), Asm. Natha Anderson (D, 20), Sen. Heidi Seevers Gansert (R, 19), Sen. Roberta Lange (D, 19), Sen. Moises Denis (D, 18).
- **Education is unusually cross-party: 55 policy bills had both parties among sponsors; 28 became law.**
- Of 193 first-committee deaths: 127 in an Education committee, 40 in Finance/Ways and Means, 12 in Government Affairs.
- **The veto wall is new and selective:** 13 vetoes, all 2023 (8) and 2025 (5) — bargaining expansions, the substitute insurance subsidy, universal free meals, summer-school mandates, children's mental-health governance (one after 42-0/20-0), sex-ed opt-out, school immigration limits, two library bills. Raises, audits, funding acts, and pipeline bills were signed.

## Deliberation prompts

1. The independent-auditor idea died seven times in first committees under sponsors from both parties, while the Legislature's own audit bill passed 42-0. Is the sticking point auditing, or who controls the auditor?
2. Recess reached the Legislature once and never got a vote; financial-literacy content passed unanimously. What makes a classroom-time mandate different from a classroom-content mandate?
3. The growth-weighting bill died in committee while test-trimming passed unanimously. Is there a constituency for changing what is measured, not just how much?
4. Two social-emotional measurement bills cleared committee and died on the Senate floor without a vote. What happens between committee and floor?
5. The 2023 raise money passed 21-0 and 41-1; the $45,000 salary floor never left committee. What does that pair say about how pay moves in Carson City?
6. AB386 passed 40-0 and 21-0 and died on a concurrence technicality. Is that a "no" on alternative reading assessments — or a process accident inviting a refile?
7. Thirteen vetoes hit bargaining, health governance and libraries; raises, audits and funding were signed. Which of the ten proposals sit on the signed side of that line?
8. Fifty-five education bills had cross-party sponsors and 28 became law. Which proposals could plausibly attract that pairing next?
9. Every enacted counselor law says "to the extent money is available," and the bill deleting those words died. Is the fight over the mandate or the money?
10. School-board restructuring died in committee three sessions running before a nonvoting-member compromise passed. Does incrementalism read as a path or a dead end here?

## Data limits carried forward

The set is broad but not a proven complete universe; the record shows where
bills stopped, never why (no veto messages or floor debate in the dataset);
committee Yeas are partly inferred and marked; party labels are 97.9%
(ballots) / 99.6% (sponsors) from official rosters; resolutions carry no
final NELIS action; special-session facts come from a manual verification
file, with the 31st (2020) AB3 education cuts verified from the enrolled
bill text.
