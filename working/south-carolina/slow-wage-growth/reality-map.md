## 3b) Proposal reality cards

### [P-age-bracketed-minimum-wage] Age-bracketed minimum wage with a separate youth wage

- **What citizens proposed:** a higher minimum wage for adults (18+) with a
  separate, lower youth wage for teens.
- **What lawmakers have tried:** nothing in this record. No bill from
  2019–2026 proposes a youth wage, training wage, or any age-bracketed
  minimum; "youth wage" and "training wage" return zero hits in all bill
  text. There is also no general minimum wage to bracket: South Carolina has
  never enacted a state minimum, and Section 41-1-110 preempts local ones.
- **Where similar ideas stopped:** the parent idea (any state minimum wage)
  stops in first committee every time — 24 bills, zero votes.
- **Nearest record:** the one wage-floor distinction the legislature enacted
  runs the other direction: 124:S533 (Act 209 of 2022) *banned* paying a
  subminimum wage to workers with disabilities, unanimously in the Senate.
- **Levers and venue:** a state-legislature lever; federal law (FLSA) already
  allows a federal youth training wage ($4.25 first 90 days) — a state
  bracket would interact with that.
- **Open questions for a group:** would a youth bracket be attached to a new
  state minimum (which has never had a hearing) or stand alone? What would
  the age line be? How does it square with the enacted policy direction of
  S533 (against subminimum tiers)?

### [P-workforce-development] Apprenticeships, career pathways, and technical training

- **What citizens proposed:** expand apprenticeships, career pathways, and
  technical-college training so workers can move into higher-paying jobs.
- **What lawmakers have tried:** the most active and most successful lane in
  the record — 5 of the record's 8 core enactments: SC WINS scholarships
  (124:H3144, Act 204 of 2022), the workforce coordinating council
  (124:H4766, Act 194 of 2022), Earn and Learn (125:H3605, Act 13 of 2023),
  the Statewide Education and Workforce Development Act (125:H3726, Act 67 of
  2023), the bigger apprentice tax credit (125:S557, Act 188 of 2024), and
  the STEM Opportunity Act (126:H3863, Act 142 of 2026). All passed with
  near-unanimous floor votes.
- **Where similar ideas stopped:** the second chamber's committee, late in the
  session: H3576 (WINS 1.0) at the Senate calendar in 2019–20; H3759/S419
  when COVID ended the 2020 session; H4060 in Senate Education; S546/S279
  (DEW restructuring) and S859 (skills-based hiring) in House committees.
- **Who has carried adjacent bills:** Rep. G.M. Smith, Sen. Davis, Speaker
  Lucas (facts only; party labels not attached in this pack).
- **Levers and venue:** state legislature plus the budget: the technical
  college board's apprenticeship lead-agency role, WINS lottery funding
  ($17M–$93.7M a year), and workforce-needs funding all live in one-year
  budget provisos, not permanent statute.
- **Open questions:** is the remaining gap funding scale, employer take-up,
  or completion outcomes? Which parts should move from one-year provisos to
  permanent law?

### [P-raise-minimum-wage] Raise the minimum wage / livable wage

- **What citizens proposed:** raise the state minimum wage toward a livable
  wage (mixed consensus; small-business cost concerns).
- **What lawmakers have tried:** 24 bills across all four sessions — fixed
  floors from $10 to $17/hr, phased schedules with inflation adjustment
  (the "SC Minimum Wage Act" family), two advisory-referendum joint
  resolutions (124:S633, 125:S28), one constitutional amendment (123:S147),
  and one wage-board mechanism (126:H3735).
- **Where similar ideas stopped:** first committee, every time, with no
  recorded vote at any stage in eight years. House Labor, Commerce and
  Industry and House Ways and Means; Senate Labor, Commerce and Industry and
  Senate Judiciary.
- **Who has carried them:** Rep. Cobb-Hunter, Sen. Scott, Rep. Garvin,
  Rep. Hart, Sen. Jackson — refiled by the same members each session.
- **Levers and venue:** state legislature (the federal $7.25 floor applies;
  41-1-110 blocks local minimums, so cities cannot act on their own).
- **Open questions:** what changed circumstances would earn the first
  committee hearing? Is the advisory-referendum route (S633/S28) a different
  path or the same chokepoint? Does the small-business concern map onto the
  incentive design in [P-employer-living-wage-incentives]?

### [P-employer-living-wage-incentives] Government incentives/bonuses for employers paying a living wage

- **What citizens proposed:** state incentives or bonuses for employers who
  pay a living wage (funding mechanism disputed).
- **What lawmakers have tried:** the exact design exists as a live bill:
  126:H4603, the Small Business Livable Wage Tax Credit Act (income tax
  credit for small employers paying at or above a livable wage), filed 2025,
  died in House Ways and Means without a vote when the session ended.
  126:H4245 (credits for hiring people leaving public assistance) sits the
  same way. 124:S398 (wage/job obligations on subsidy recipients, with
  clawbacks) died in Senate LCI.
- **Where adjacent ideas succeed:** wage-linked employer incentives pass
  when they ride tax packages: job development credits (which key payouts
  to wage levels) were updated by 124:S901 (Act 237 of 2022) and 125:H4087
  (Act 222 of 2024); the apprentice hiring credit grew via 125:S557
  (Act 188 of 2024). All near-unanimous.
- **Who has carried adjacent bills:** H4603 (prefiled December 2025,
  introduced January 2026) and H4245 were both led by Rep. Jones;
  tax-package vehicles run through House Ways and Means and Senate Finance.
- **Levers and venue:** state legislature (tax code); the disputed funding
  question is a Ways and Means/Finance question in practice.
- **Open questions:** would the credit be refundable and how is "livable
  wage" defined and updated? Does the design ride a committee tax package
  (the route that passes) or stand alone (the route that has not had a
  vote)? What take-up would small employers actually see?

## 5) Deliberation prompts (questions, not answers)

1. If minimum-wage bills have stopped in the same first committees 24 times
   without a hearing, what would need to change for hearing number one?
2. Is a committee death a "no" on the idea, or a "no" on the vehicle? The
   Employment First bills died twice before S533 passed unanimously.
3. When a design keeps passing one chamber unanimously and dying in the
   other's committee (skills-based hiring, DEW restructuring), what does the
   second chamber need to see?
4. The one recorded floor defeat (H3368, overtime tax exemption, 16–27 in
   the Senate) followed a 121–0 House vote. What made the chambers read the
   same idea so differently?
5. Workforce-pathway laws pass almost unanimously. Does that mean the lane
   is open for more, or that the easy pieces are done?
6. Much of the apprenticeship and scholarship machinery lives in one-year
   budget provisos. Should any of it be permanent law, and who decides that?
7. A livable-wage tax credit exists as a filed bill that never got a vote.
   Is the obstacle the design, the definition of "livable," or the fiscal
   note?
8. No youth/training wage has ever been filed. Is that because no member has
   championed it, or because the parent policy (a state minimum) has no
   traction?
