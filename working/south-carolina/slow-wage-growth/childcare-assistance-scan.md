# Child care assistance — legislative record scan (2019–2026)

Requested 2026-09-04 in connection with discussions with sitting legislators
about possible child care assistance. Method: full-text and title/summary
scan of the certified universe (all 15,817 instruments, 123rd–126th General
Assemblies) for child care / day care / early childhood / First Steps /
dependent care / pre-K terms, policy instruments only; plus a caption scan of
all six enacted Part IB proviso sets. 68 candidate bills reviewed; the
issue-relevant set is below. Facts verbatim from official bill texts,
histories, and Part IB; no committee tallies exist anywhere (SC publishes
outcomes only); sponsors from official sponsor lines, no party labels
(no roster join).

## What became law (statute)

| Bill | Act | What it did |
|---|---|---|
| S595 (2019) | Act 52 of 2019 | Background-check rules for childcare facility staff |
| H4023 (2023) | Act 81 of 2023 | Restructured local First Steps partnership boards |
| S862 (2024) | Act 216 of 2024 | Caregiver education and preservice-training requirements for childcare centers |

Pattern: what passes in statute is **regulation and governance**, not
affordability or assistance.

## The near-miss (high support, not enacted)

- **S946 (2024, Sen. Shealy) — childcare regulations modernization.** Passed
  the Senate on a 45–0 roll call (2/21/2024), passed the House 105–0 amended
  (5/8/2024), Senate non-concurred, conference committee appointed 5/9/2024,
  died when the session ended. The same died-in-conference path as the
  Employment First bill (H3244, 2021), whose content passed the next year.

## Assistance/affordability designs — filed, never voted on

Every direct-assistance design died in committee without a recorded vote:

- **H4394 (2025, Rep. J.L. Johnson):** DSS + DEW workforce-development
  childcare **stipends for unemployed parents and caregivers** of children
  under 12 — the design closest to "childcare assistance tied to getting
  people into work." Died in House Ways and Means.
- **S47 (2025, Sens. Davis, Sutton, Devine, Graham) / H4015 (2025, Rep.
  Henderson-Myers + 8):** companion bills to **raise the cap on the existing
  employer childcare-program tax credit (§12-6-3440)** and apply it against
  more taxes, plus a **new income tax credit for childcare directors and
  staff** (§12-6-3595 — a pay supplement for childcare workers through the
  tax code). Died in Senate Finance / House Ways and Means.
- **H5794 (filed August 2026, Rep. J.L. Johnson):** make the existing child
  and dependent care income tax credit (§12-6-3380) **refundable**. Referred
  to House Ways and Means 8/11/2026 — the newest bill in this scan, pending
  as of the 8/26/2026 collection date.
- **H4993 (2024, Rep. J. Moore + 4):** the Childcare Advance Act — deferral
  of a portion of income tax against eligible childcare expenses. House Ways
  and Means.
- **H5205 (2024, Rep. J. Moore):** High-Quality Prekindergarten Expansion
  grant program at the Department of Education. House Ways and Means.
- **H3079 (2021, Reps. Collins and Cobb-Hunter):** a study committee on the
  availability of high-quality childcare statewide. House Education and
  Public Works.
- **S770 (2026, Sen. Kimbrell):** the "Childcare Assistance Program" bill —
  runs the other direction: adds **employment requirements** as a condition
  of federally funded childcare assistance. Senate Family and Veterans'
  Services.
- **S291 (2019, 123rd):** would have created a Department of Early
  Development and Education consolidating early-care programs. First
  committee.
- Regulation/quality bills still in the pipeline when the 126th ended:
  H4632 (Cash Berry Childcare Safety and Quality Rating Act), S700/H4587
  (military-installation childcare exemptions), plus First Steps agency
  bills (S1115, H5111, S1170, 2022) that preceded the enacted H4023.

## Existing law and budget machinery (the base the bills build on)

- **Two childcare tax credits already exist in statute:** the employer
  childcare-program credit (§12-6-3440 — S47/H4015 would enlarge it) and the
  individual child & dependent care credit (§12-6-3380 — H5794 would make it
  refundable). Any new incentive would extend, not invent, this machinery.
- **The assistance program itself is federal money run through DSS** (the SC
  child care scholarship/voucher program). The budget regulates it annually:
  proviso 38.17 (DSS: Child Care Voucher — vouchers must be used at
  providers exceeding minimum licensing standards; present in all six
  enacted cycles) and, new in FY 2026-27, proviso 38.28 (DSS: Childcare
  Provider Fraud reporting).
- **State pre-K expands through the budget, not statute:** Full-Day 4K /
  CERDEP provisos appear in every enacted cycle (SDE 1.46, SDE-EIA 1A.20,
  1A.25, 1A.43, 1A.48, 1A.71 in FY 2026-27), and First Steps' move into the
  Department of Education ran through provisos (1.97 in FY 2023-24, 1.109 in
  FY 2024-25).

## What the record indicates (descriptive, basket language)

1. **The childcare record has the same shape as the wage record.**
   Regulation and governance bills move (three acts plus S946's unanimous
   double passage); direct assistance and affordability designs sit in the
   money committees without votes — none has ever received one. The
   chokepoint is House Ways and Means / Senate Finance, the same rooms that
   hold the livable-wage credit.
2. **Signals that distinguish it from the minimum-wage pattern (where
   nothing has moved in 24 tries):**
   - Every assistance design filed uses the **tax code** — the one venue
     where wage-linked incentives do pass (the apprentice-credit raise, S557,
     passed 42–0 and 111–0 in 2024; S47 is structurally the same move —
     raising an existing credit).
   - **Interest is accelerating**: four childcare bills in the 123rd vs.
     eight-plus in the 126th, with a brand-new refundable-credit bill filed
     in August 2026.
   - **Both directions are filing** — expansion designs (stipends, credits,
     pre-K grants) and a conditions design (S770's work requirements) — and
     the sponsor lists span the chamber's frequent workforce carriers:
     Sen. Davis (lead on S47) also carried the enacted earned-wage-access
     law and the 42–0 skills-based hiring bill.
   - **S946 shows childcare bills can win unanimous floor votes** when they
     reach the floor; its death was a conference-timing stop, the same stop
     that preceded Employment First's enactment a year later.
3. **What has never been filed:** a state-funded expansion of childcare
   assistance beyond the federal voucher money, and any direct-payment
   (non-tax-code) subsidy to families or providers. The stipend design
   (H4394) has been filed once and never heard.
4. **Connection to the final proposal grid:** the S47/H4015 employer-credit
   design and the §12-6-3595 childcare-worker credit sit in the same family
   as the grid's employer living-wage incentive (tax-code incentives decided
   in the same two committees), and H4394's stipends tie childcare to
   workforce participation — the grid's workforce-development lane.
